How do you make a social media content calendar?

Decide how many times a week you'll post, which platforms you'll post on and your mix of formats (reels, carousels, static posts). Block out the days and dates you won't post. Then spread the posts evenly across the month and give each one a format and a content pillar. This generator does that layout in one click, and exports a CSV you can open in Google Sheets or Excel or hand to your team.
🔒Your plan stays on your device. The calendar and CSV are built in your browser, and nothing is uploaded or stored. (The page uses Google Analytics for anonymous page-view counts, like the rest of this site.)

How to use the content calendar generator

  1. Pick the month and year, and how many posts a week you can produce consistently.
  2. Tick the platforms. Each post is planned as a cross-post to all of them. Delete platforms per row in the sheet if a post is platform-specific.
  3. Set the format mix. With weights of 40 / 30 / 30, about 40% of posts become reels. Formats are interleaved so you don't get three reels in a row.
  4. Skip days and dates: weekends, festivals, launch-day blackouts, the client's holidays. Posts move to the remaining days in that week, and carry into the next week if a week is full.
  5. Add content pillars (optional) and they rotate through the month.
  6. Download the CSV and fill in topics and captions with your team, or print the month for a planning meeting.

Opening the CSV in Google Sheets or Excel

  • Google Sheets: File → Import → Upload, choose the downloaded file, then pick "Insert new sheet(s)" or "Replace current sheet".
  • Excel: open the file directly. It is saved as UTF-8 with a byte-order mark, so ₹ and non-English pillar names display correctly.
  • Columns: Date, Day, Time, Platforms, Format, Content pillar, Topic / hook, Caption, Status. Topic, caption and status are left for your team to fill in.

Frequently asked questions

How many posts per week should I plan?

There is no universal number. Plan a frequency your team can deliver every week at the same quality. Consistency matters more than volume, and you can raise the number next month once the workflow holds.

Does the CSV work in Google Sheets and Excel?

Yes. It is a standard comma-separated file saved as UTF-8 with a byte-order mark. In Google Sheets use File, Import, Upload. In Excel, open the file directly.

How does the generator spread posts across the month?

It works out a monthly total from your posts per week and the length of the month, then splits it across the calendar weeks in proportion to how many postable (not skipped) days each week has. Inside each week, posts go on evenly spaced days that you haven't skipped. If skipped days leave a week too full, the extra posts carry into the next week.

Can I skip festivals or holidays?

Yes. Type the day numbers into "Skip specific dates", for example 2, 15, 26, and those dates stay empty. Whole weekdays, such as Sundays, can be skipped with the day checkboxes.

Is my calendar saved anywhere?

No. The calendar and the CSV are generated in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing is kept after you close the page unless you download the CSV.
