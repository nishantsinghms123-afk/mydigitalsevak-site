Hootsuite Alternative in India: What ₹999 Buys That $99 Doesn't

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published August 2, 2026

Hootsuite's own pricing page lists paid plans from $99 for Standard to $199 Professional and $399 Advanced — per user, per month, on annual billing, excluding taxes. For an Indian agency the two killers are that it bills per seat and that client approvals only appear on the top tier. Rupee-priced tools do the same job for a tenth of the cost.

Every Indian agency owner I know has had the same Hootsuite moment. You sign up for the trial, the product is genuinely good, and then you open the billing page, multiply by the exchange rate, and realise the tool costs more than what you charge two of your clients combined.

This post does the rupee math properly, using figures taken directly from each vendor's own pricing page rather than the American listicles. And it flags one cost that no "Hootsuite alternatives" article I've read mentions: the GST you pay on a foreign software subscription.

What does Hootsuite actually cost?

Straight from hootsuite.com/plans, in its own words: "Hootsuite pricing is tiered based on your plan type. Paid plans start at $99 for a Standard plan, $199 for a Professional plan, and range up to $399 for an Advanced plan."

Two structural details matter far more than the headline numbers:

  • It's per seat. The page states plainly that "Hootsuite plans are sold per user (or 'seat'), so you can add as many users as your organization needs." Its comparison table lists user seats as "1+" on every paid tier. Your bill multiplies by headcount, not by clients.
  • Approvals are on the top tier. Standard covers "up to 10 social media accounts"; Professional adds unlimited accounts. "Review and approve content with your team" appears only under Advanced — the $399 line. For an agency, approval is the workflow, so the relevant price is the highest one.

The page also carries a footnote worth reading: prices are "based on annual billing, but do not include applicable taxes." Monthly billing costs more than the annual rate, and the trial has limits — Hootsuite notes trial accounts have daily posting caps of 10–20 posts per organisation, and bulk scheduling is unavailable during it. So a two-week trial won't tell you how the tool behaves on a month-in-a-day batching workflow.

Honesty note on currency: Hootsuite's plan cards inject the currency symbol client-side, so I could not verify from the page source what figure an Indian visitor's card renders. The dollar amounts quoted above are the ones Hootsuite states server-side in its own FAQ. Check the live page from your own browser before budgeting — that's the number you'll actually be charged.

The 18% nobody puts in the comparison

When an Indian business buys software from a foreign supplier, that's an import of service. The liability generally shifts to you: as ClearTax puts it, "the service recipient in India is liable to pay GST under the reverse charge mechanism", and "for most professional services, GST is usually charged at 18% on the value of the service received."

The good news is that "such GST paid shall be available to the service recipient as an input tax credit, subject to other applicable terms" — so if you're GST-registered, you claim it back. The catch is cash flow: you fund the 18% now and recover it in the return cycle. And if you're not registered, the same source notes an unregistered entity importing services for business purposes must obtain registration under Section 24(iii) of the CGST Act before it can pay the reverse-charge GST at all. A ₹15,000-a-month tool subscription can be the thing that drags a small studio into registration. What are you actually giving up?

Being fair matters more than being persuasive, so: Hootsuite is not overpriced for what it is. It's an enterprise social suite, and the things it does that cheaper tools genuinely don't include social listening across a wide surface, a unified inbox with automated reply routing, message assignment to team members, trend forecasting, and governance features like SSO on Enterprise. If you're a 40-person agency servicing listed companies with compliance requirements, those line items are the product and the price is defensible.

But run the honest audit of your own month. For most Indian agencies under 20 people, the actual daily jobs are: plan the calendar, get the client to approve, publish on time, pull the numbers, send the report, raise the invoice, chase the payment. Social listening is a feature people demo and then never open. You are, in practice, paying enterprise prices for a calendar.

Which alternative should an Indian agency pick?

  1. You just need to publish, cheaply, to many accounts. SocialPilot at ₹2,000 (or ₹6,000 with approvals) is India-priced and hard to beat. Zoho Social's agency tiers are worth a look too — Zoho is India-headquartered and its page states pricing readjusts by region, though the figures render dynamically so check your own INR number.
  2. Your team is large but your channel count is small. Buffer Team's unlimited-users-per-channel model inverts Hootsuite's per-seat economics. Expensive per channel, free per human.
  3. You're only just outgrowing free tools. Don't jump to a paid suite yet — read Meta Business Suite vs scheduling tools first and make sure you've actually hit the ceiling.
  4. Scheduling was never your bottleneck. Then no scheduler on this list is your answer, cheap or expensive.

How do you switch without breaking the calendar?

  1. Switch at a month boundary, never mid-campaign. Your reporting cycle stays clean.
  2. Export your scheduled queue first, as a CSV or at minimum a screenshot of the next 30 days. Queues rarely migrate between tools.
  3. Reconnect accounts through the official Meta flow, not by sharing passwords — see auto-posting without password sharing.
  4. Keep the old tool alive for one overlapping month. A silent publish failure during migration costs a client relationship worth far more than the subscription.
  5. Check your annual commitment. If you're mid-term on annual billing, you're paying for the overlap anyway — plan the switch for renewal month.
