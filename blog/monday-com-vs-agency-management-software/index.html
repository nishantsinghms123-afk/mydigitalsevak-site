monday.com vs Agency Management Software: The Config Tax

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published September 13, 2026

monday.com is a superb board engine and a generic one. It will model an agency's work if you build the model yourself — and that build, plus its maintenance, is the config tax. It cannot publish a post, cannot raise a GST invoice, and its 250-action automation cap on Standard pushes most agencies to Pro within a quarter.

Nearly every agency that outgrows a spreadsheet lands on monday.com, Asana or Notion. monday wins that bake-off more often than the other two, and for a real reason: the board metaphor is closer to how agency work actually behaves than a task list is. Columns for client, format, designer, status, due date — it looks like your job on day one.

This isn't a hit piece. We ran a monday board for eleven months and some of it was genuinely better than what we replaced it with. What the comparison listicles won't tell you is where the bill lands and which specific capability you'll go looking for in month four and not find. So here's that, with monday's own published numbers converted to rupees.

What does monday.com genuinely do well for an agency?

Three things, and the first one is better than most purpose-built agency tools manage.

  • Cross-client visibility in one view. A dashboard that pulls twenty boards into a single "what's late this week" widget is monday's real product. Most social-media tools show you one brand at a time; monday shows you the agency. If your pain is "I don't know what's slipping", monday fixes it fastest.
  • The automation recipe builder is genuinely usable by non-technical staff. Your account manager can build "when status changes to Approved, notify designer and move to This Week" without a developer. That is rarer than it sounds.
  • It bends to any process. Odd workflow? Three-stage approval with a regional-language review step? monday will model it. No opinionated tool will.

So the real question isn't "is monday.com good". It's whether a tool that has to be told what a client, a post and an invoice are is the right foundation for a business made entirely of clients, posts and invoices.

What does monday.com actually cost an Indian agency?

Two things make monday's price harder to estimate than the sticker: seats are sold in blocks, not one at a time, and the tier you think you need usually isn't the one you end up on.

From monday.com's pricing page as of today, Work Management is $0 for up to 2 seats (Free), then $9, $12 and $19 per seat per month on Basic, Standard and Pro, all billed yearly — the page's own toggle advertises an 18% saving for paying annually, so monthly billing costs meaningfully more. Every paid tier carries a three-seat minimum, and the seat selector steps in blocks (3, 5, 10, 15, 20) rather than letting you type your exact headcount.

That block structure is where agencies get surprised. A four-person team buys five seats. A six-person team buys ten. At ₹88 to the dollar:

Why Standard is usually a trap

monday's pricing page puts 250 automation actions and 250 integration actions per month on Standard, against 25,000 of each on Pro. Read that as an agency operator: every status change that fires a recipe, every date reminder, every "notify the designer" burns an action. Across twelve clients posting daily, 250 actions is about a week. You either turn the automations off — which removes the reason you bought monday — or you move to Pro. Budget for Pro from the start; it's the honest number.

Where does a monday.com board stop being enough?

Five points, in the order they arrive.

1. The board describes the post. It doesn't publish it.

monday.com has no native Instagram or Facebook publishing. A row that says "Reel · 7:00 PM Friday · Scheduled" is a label; at 7:00 PM, nothing happens. Someone opens the phone, finds the export in Drive, copies the caption off the board, and posts it. You can bridge it with Zapier or a marketplace connector, but you've now added a subscription, a token that expires, and a failure mode with no alarm — the calendar keeps looking green while nothing goes out. This is the single biggest difference between a project tool and an Instagram scheduler that actually publishes.

2. Guest access solves the wrong half of the approval problem

monday's pricing page lists guest access from Standard upward, and monday's support documentation on guest pricing describes Standard guests billed at four guests per paid seat, with Pro and Enterprise allowing unlimited guests. On paper, excellent — cheaper client access than most schedulers offer.

The problem isn't the price. It's that your client has to create a monday.com account, remember it, and open a project-management tool to say "haan, theek hai". Indian clients — the restaurant owner, the jeweller, the clinic's practice manager — will not. They approve on WhatsApp at 11 PM from the phone, in a voice note, with one line of feedback that's a revision rather than a rejection. A board comment thread is not an approval record; it's a chat you'll have to scroll through in month seven when someone disputes what was signed off. We wrote up what a working version looks like in our content approval workflow template.

3. There is no money layer at all

monday.com will not raise a GST-compliant tax invoice with your GSTIN, HSN/SAC code and CGST/SGST split. It will not handle a client who deducts 10% TDS under 194J and pays you ₹45,000 against a ₹50,000 invoice. It will not collect by UPI, and it won't run a recurring mandate. You can build a board that tracks invoices — every agency does — but the invoice itself gets made somewhere else, which means your "system of record" doesn't know which client hasn't paid.

If that's your pain point specifically, the mechanics are in GST invoices for digital marketing services and recurring billing with Razorpay.

4. Reporting is about your work, not your client's results

monday's dashboards are excellent at throughput: tasks done, overdue items, workload per person. They are blind to reach, saves, follower delta and top-performing posts, because monday never touched the Instagram account. So your monthly client report is still assembled by hand from Insights screenshots on the 3rd of every month. That report is what renews the retainer — and it's the deliverable monday is furthest from helping with.

5. The config tax, which is the real cost

Here's the one nobody prices. A monday board that fits an agency doesn't come out of the box — someone builds it. Two weeks of an account manager's evenings, then continuous maintenance: a new client means duplicating a board and re-pointing the automations; a new format means a column; a departing employee means a permissions audit nobody does.

And it's fragile in a specific way — the setup lives in one person's head. When that person leaves, you inherit a system of 40 boards, 60 automations and no documentation. I've watched two agencies quietly abandon a monday workspace within six weeks of losing the person who built it. A tool that already knows what a client and a post are has no config tax, because there's no config. That trade-off is the entire argument of agency management software as a category, and it holds against Asana and Notion identically — see Asana vs agency management software and Notion vs agency management software for the same comparison from the other two directions.

When should you stay on monday.com?

Genuinely, and not as throat-clearing before a pitch:

  • You're a full-service agency, not a social-media agency. If your work is websites, events, print and video as much as feeds, publishing isn't your bottleneck and monday's generality is exactly right. Our client-portal thinking applies to social delivery, not to a brand-film shoot schedule.
  • Someone in-house loves building systems. The config tax is only a tax if nobody wants to pay it. If your ops lead enjoys this, monday will out-flex any packaged tool.
  • You already run non-marketing work in it. HR, hiring pipelines, vendor tracking — monday is a fine home for all of it, and splitting that out costs more than it saves.
  • As the layer alongside. The split that works: internal projects and cross-team workload in monday; calendar, approvals, publishing, reports and billing in a system that knows what those words mean.

The 30-second decision

  • Under 5 clients, mostly project work → monday.com Standard. Don't overthink it.
  • You publish daily for 8+ brands → you need a publisher, not a board. Keep monday only if you're also using it for non-social work.
  • Automations are your reason for buying → price Pro, not Standard. 250 actions is a week.
  • Clients dispute approvals, or deduct TDS → you need an audit trail and a billing layer. A board is neither.
  • 6th hire incoming → check the seat block before you sign. That one person can double the bill.
  • Small team in a tier-2 market → the dollar cost matters more than the feature list. Our Indore agency page and the agency management software page put the consolidated alternative in rupees.

FAQ

Can monday.com schedule and publish Instagram posts?

Not natively. monday.com has no built-in Instagram or Facebook publishing, so a board column that reads "Scheduled" is a label rather than a queue. Agencies bridge the gap with Zapier or a marketplace connector, which means one more subscription and one more thing that breaks silently when a Meta token expires.

How much does monday.com cost for a small Indian agency?

monday.com publishes Work Management at $9, $12 and $19 per seat per month on yearly billing for Basic, Standard and Pro. Seats are sold in blocks with a three-seat minimum, so a five-seat Pro plan is about $95 a month — roughly ₹8,400 before any scheduling or invoicing tool sits on top.

Can I give clients access to monday.com boards for approvals?

Yes, through guest access on Standard and above. monday's support documentation describes Standard guests billed at four guests per paid seat, with Pro and Enterprise allowing unlimited guests. The practical problem isn't the price — it's that your client has to create a monday login and actually open it.

What is the automation limit on monday.com Standard?

monday.com's pricing page lists 250 automation actions and 250 integration actions per month on Standard, rising to 25,000 of each on Pro. For an agency where every status change fires a recipe, 250 actions is roughly one busy week — which makes Pro the real starting tier.
