Invoice Software for a Digital Marketing Agency in India: The Buyer's Checklist

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published 21 Nov 2026

Invoice software for an Indian digital marketing agency must do more than make a GST PDF. It should pick CGST+SGST or IGST from the place of supply, show TDS as the net receivable, keep tax invoices and non-GST bills in separate series, draft retainers for review, chase payment on WhatsApp and email, and hand your CA GSTR-1 and TDS exports.

Every invoice app on Google's first page makes a tidy GST invoice. That was never our problem. Our problem was the 1st of the month: thirty-odd retainers to raise, a third of the clients deducting TDS, a handful in other states, two who pay in parts, one who "never got the email", and a CA asking for the GSTR-1 data by the 11th.

A generic tool treats each invoice as a document. An agency needs a monthly cycle tied to the work. So this isn't a roundup of apps; it's the checklist I'd hand any founder before a demo.

How does software decide between CGST+SGST and IGST?

By the place of supply, and for most agency work the rule is simple. Under Section 12 of the IGST Act, a service supplied to a registered person is supplied at that person's location. Same state as you: CGST 9% + SGST 9%. Another state: IGST 18%.

Our agency is in Rudrapur, Uttarakhand. A Dehradun client gets CGST + SGST; a client in Delhi NCR gets IGST. Two traps to watch:

  • Bill the GSTIN, not the contact. Your marketing manager may sit in Mumbai while the GSTIN you're billing is registered in Gujarat. The registration decides.
  • Unregistered clients still have a state. The doctor, the small café. CBIC's Circular 242/2024 called out suppliers who skip the recipient's state and default the place of supply to their own. Record the client's state, and let the software use it.

Software should store the client's state once and never ask again. If you pick the tax type on every invoice, it's a template, not a system. The invoice field list (SAC, serial rules) is in our GST invoice guide for digital marketing services.

Why should TDS show up on the invoice at all?

Because otherwise your books lie to you. ₹50,000 retainer + ₹9,000 GST = ₹59,000. The client deducts 2% TDS on the pre-GST value, ₹1,000, and pays ₹58,000. A generic app now shows ₹1,000 outstanding, forever. Across twenty clients your "pending" number is fiction.

The invoice should carry the expected TDS and show the net receivable, and "paid" should mean gross minus TDS. The ₹1,000 is tax credit under your PAN to reconcile against Form 26AS, not a debt. Which rate applies (1%, 2% or 10%) is covered in our TDS on digital marketing services guide; read it before you set a default.

A-series tax invoices, B-series bills

Most agencies bill something outside GST: a reimbursement, an arrangement your CA has approved, a separate line of business. If those share the tax-invoice sequence, invoice 014 is followed by 016 and somebody asks where 015 went. Keep tax invoices in an A-series and non-GST bills in a B-series, and ask whether a tool does this on its own.

Retainers that draft themselves, with a human in the loop

A retainer that auto-sends ₹40,000 after the client cut scope to ₹30,000 is worse than no automation. The pattern that works: the system drafts retainer invoices a couple of days early, someone approves, edits or skips each one, and it goes out on billing day with last month's report attached. Clients pay faster when the invoice and the proof arrive together. If you want card or UPI auto-debit on top, read our breakdown of recurring billing with Razorpay and the ₹15,000 mandate limit first. TDS clients complicate auto-debit more than vendors admit. And if you bill retainers in advance, GST on advance payments changes when the tax is due.

Reminders that get read, and payment where the client already is

We tried polite emails for two years. What moved money was a fixed ladder on WhatsApp as well as email: before the due date, on it, then a few days, a week and two weeks after, then weekly. At two weeks a human calls, and software should create that task, because nobody remembers. The scripts and the MSME 45-day angle are in how to handle late-paying agency clients.

Then make paying easy. Our clients already open the client app to approve posts, so they pay there too.

What does your CA need from the software every month?

GSTR-1 is due by the 11th of the following month for monthly filers, or the 13th after the quarter for QRMP filers (ClearTax's due-date summary; always confirm extensions on gst.gov.in). Software should make that a download:

  • A GSTR-1-ready CSV of the period's tax invoices
  • A TDS register: client, invoice, amount deducted, for the quarterly 26AS check
  • A bulk ZIP of invoice PDFs
  • A read-only login for the CA or accounts person, so they can check figures without being able to edit an invoice

Where a dedicated accounting tool still wins

If you only need GST-correct invoices and full books, an accounting product (Zoho Books, Tally and the like) is the right tool, and your CA probably lives in one. Agency software is worth it when you want billing joined to the work. Also check e-invoicing: once aggregate turnover crosses ₹5 crore you must register invoices on the government portal, and ad spend billed through your own invoices counts towards it. Frequently asked questions

Can I use a generic invoice app for my digital marketing agency in India?

Yes, if it is GST-aware and you are disciplined. A generic app makes a correct PDF. What it usually does not do is show TDS as the net you will receive, draft retainers before the billing day, chase payment on WhatsApp, or link the invoice to the work and the report the client is paying for.

Should my agency invoice show CGST and SGST or IGST?

It depends on the place of supply. For a GST-registered client it is the client's registered location: same state as you means CGST 9% plus SGST 9%, another state means IGST 18%. For an unregistered client, record their state on the invoice and use their location. Good software works this out from the client's state.

What should my CA get from agency invoicing software every month?

Three things: a GSTR-1 ready export of the month's tax invoices, a TDS register showing what each client deducted against which invoice, and a bulk download of the invoice PDFs. Ideally the CA also gets a read-only login, so they can check figures themselves without being able to edit an invoice.
