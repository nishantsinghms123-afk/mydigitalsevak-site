Agorapulse Alternative for Small Agencies: The Seat Maths

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published September 9, 2026

Agorapulse charges per user: $99 a month per seat on Standard, $79 on annual billing, rising to $149 a seat on Advanced. For a five-person Indian agency that is roughly ₹52,000 to ₹66,000 a month before 18% GST under reverse charge. The alternative you want is not a cheaper scheduler — it is a tool that charges on a different axis.

Agorapulse is a good product. I am not going to pretend otherwise to sell you ours. The problem small agencies run into is not quality, it is the shape of the price: it scales with the size of your team, and an Indian agency adds people faster than it adds revenue per client. A designer and an editor cost you a fraction of what they cost in London, which is exactly why you hire them — and then the tool charges you a Western seat price for each one.

So this post does the sum properly, from Agorapulse's own price card, and then sorts the alternatives by which pricing axis they use. If you have read the Sprout Social post, the reverse-charge logic is identical; the difference is that Agorapulse is cheap enough per seat that agencies do not notice the multiplier until they are eight people in.

Why does per-user pricing punish a small agency?

Because Agorapulse bills you on two axes that both grow, and they are the two axes an agency grows on. Seats grow with your team. Profiles grow with your client list. A tool priced per client would at least track your revenue. A tool priced per seat tracks your payroll, which is the one number you are trying to make bigger without your costs following.

Here is the maths for an agency of five — founder, account manager, designer, editor, one intern who schedules. Everyone touches the calendar, so everyone needs a login. I have used ₹88 to the dollar as a round figure; swap today's rate and the shape holds.

Two things fall out of that table. First, the tier you actually need is not the entry tier. Agencies buy this category of tool for client sign-off, and approvals do not exist on Standard — they start on Professional, and the multi-step version with shared calendars starts on Advanced. So the honest starting price for agency use is $119 to $149 a seat on annual billing, not $79.

Second, the profile cap bites earlier than you expect. Ten profiles sounds roomy until you count properly: six clients running Instagram and Facebook is twelve profiles, so you are paying $30 a month in overage before anyone mentions a YouTube channel or a Google Business Profile. It is not a large number. It is just a second meter running.

Then add tax. A foreign SaaS subscription is an OIDAR service for GST. If you are registered you self-assess 18% IGST under reverse charge and normally recover it as input credit — a cash-flow cost rather than a permanent one, but it leaves the bank first. If you are not registered, the 18% is simply gone. We walked through that in the Hootsuite post and the GST invoicing guide.

Set the annual figure against what you bill. Most small-city retainers in our pricing survey sit between ₹15,000 and ₹50,000 a month. In Indore or Lucknow, the five-seat Professional plan is one to three whole retainers a year, paid to a tool. In Mumbai the sum is easier, but it is the same sum.

What is Agorapulse genuinely good at?

The inbox. If your agency does community management — replying to comments and DMs across several brands, assigning conversations, moderating comments on live ad campaigns, keeping an audit trail of who answered what — Agorapulse is one of the best tools in the category and the seat price starts to look reasonable, because a seat maps to a person actually working a queue all day.

The reporting is also client-ready out of the box, and the ROI reporting on Advanced is real, not decorative. If you sell social as a performance channel and you have to defend spend in a monthly meeting, that has value.

Where it stops is where most Indian content agencies live. It does not invoice. It does not track GST or TDS. There is no client-facing portal in the sense a brand owner in India expects — a link on their phone that opens the month's posts and lets them approve or ask for a change, without a login they will forget. And it charges you per person for a workflow where most of your people are producing, not conversing.

A per-seat tool is priced for an in-house team at a brand, where headcount is stable and the tool is a cost centre. An agency is the opposite: headcount is the thing you flex to serve more clients. If your tool taxes the flex, it is quietly voting against you growing. That is the argument the whole agency management software page is built on, and it is also why we cap nothing by user.

One caveat before you switch on price alone. If you have two people and both work the inbox all day, Agorapulse Professional at two seats is about ₹21,000 a month and probably worth it. The seat model only becomes hostile when the number of people who need to see the calendar is much larger than the number who need to work it. Count those two groups separately before you decide — the answer is different for a community-management shop and a content shop, and we wrote about where that line sits in how many clients one manager can handle.

How do you move without losing a month?

  1. Export before the renewal, not after. Pull your next 30 days of scheduled posts and the last three months of reports while the account is still live. Annual plans do not give you a grace period to go back for them.
  2. Reconnect through the official API. Whatever you move to should connect Instagram and Facebook via Meta's API with the client as page admin, never a shared password. Reasons in the password-sharing post.
  3. Run one client in parallel for two weeks. Same posts, both tools, on your least dramatic client. If the approval loop and the publishing hold, move the rest.
  4. Rebuild the approval habit, not just the calendar. The migration that fails is the one where the client never learns the new approve button. Send one WhatsApp message with a screenshot. That is the whole change-management plan.
  5. Diarise the cancellation. Set a reminder 30 days before renewal. On a five-seat annual plan, a missed cancellation is a ₹6 lakh mistake.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Agorapulse cost per user?

Agorapulse's own pricing page lists Standard at $99 a user a month or $79 on annual billing, Professional at $149 or $119 annual, and Advanced at $199 or $149 annual, with a Custom tier on quote. All three paid tiers include 10 social profiles and extra profiles cost $15 a month each.

Why is Agorapulse expensive for a small agency?

Because it charges on two axes at once. The seat price multiplies by every teammate who needs a login, and profiles beyond the included ten are billed separately as you add clients. A small agency grows on both axes at the same time, so the bill compounds faster than the client list.

Does Agorapulse have client approval workflows?

Approvals are tier-gated. Standard has no approval workflow, Professional adds simple approvals, and multi-step approval workflows plus five shared calendars start on Advanced. So an agency that buys Agorapulse specifically for client sign-off is really shopping at the $119 to $149 a user tiers, not the entry price.
