GST Invoice for Digital Marketing Services: SAC, 18% GST and Format

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published July 24, 2026 · Updated 3 Oct 2026

A GST invoice for digital marketing services in India charges 18% GST under SAC 998361 (advertising and marketing services) — CGST 9% + SGST 9% when the client is in your state, IGST 18% when they are in another. It must show both GSTINs, a unique serial number, the place of supply and the taxable value, and go out within 30 days of the service.

I run an agency that invoices retainers every month, and I'll say the quiet part first: GST for a services agency is genuinely simple — one rate, one or two SAC codes, no inventory complications. Agencies get it wrong not because the law is hard but because nobody explains it in agency terms. This is the explanation I wish I'd had before our first registered invoice. (I'm a founder, not a CA — treat this as a working map and confirm your specifics with a professional.)

Do you even need to register?

Registration becomes mandatory when aggregate turnover crosses ₹20 lakh in a financial year (₹10 lakh in special-category states). Below that it's optional — but two things push agencies to register early. First, serious business clients prefer a GST invoice because the 18% comes back to them as input tax credit; an unregistered freelancer bill sits worse with their accountant than a proper tax invoice. Second, once registered you claim credit on your own inputs — software subscriptions, laptops, that co-working desk. We covered how GST interacts with your pricing in how much to charge for social media management: always quote "₹40,000 + GST", never an inclusive number.

One myth worth killing: a client in another state does not, by itself, force a services agency to register. Notification 10/2017–Integrated Tax exempts people making inter-State supplies of taxable services from registration while their all-India aggregate turnover stays within ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special-category states). The flip side: until you register you cannot charge GST at all, so your bill carries no tax line and the client gets no input credit.

Which SAC code goes on a digital marketing invoice?

Services use SAC (Services Accounting Codes) the way goods use HSN. For digital marketing there are two you'll actually use, both taxed at 18% (Busy's 2026 rate guide is a solid reference):

SAC codeCoversTypical agency line items
998361Advertising services — planning, creating and placing ads, campaign management (the general agency bucket)Social media management retainer, SEO, content marketing, influencer coordination, branding, Google / Meta ads management fee
998365Sale of internet advertising space or time (not on commission)Selling ad slots on a website, app, newsletter or page you own; reselling media inventory as the seller

The practical split: if you are paid to plan, create or run campaigns, it is 998361 — including the fee for managing a client's Meta or Google ads. 998365 is for when you are the one selling the ad space. Since April 2021, B2B invoices must carry at least a 4-digit SAC, and 6 digits once aggregate turnover crosses ₹5 crore (Notification 78/2020–Central Tax, as summarised on the e-invoice portal). In practice: put the full 6-digit code on every invoice and never think about it again. The code doesn't change the 18% — it changes how cleanly your filings match your client's.

And the 18% survived the September 2025 rate overhaul. The 56th GST Council's service-rate changes, effective 22 September 2025, touched heading 9983 only for services tied to petroleum and natural-gas exploration, mining or drilling — advertising and marketing stayed at 18% with input tax credit.

What must the invoice itself contain?

The legal list lives in Rule 46 of the CGST Rules (ClearTax's explainer walks through it). For an agency billing services, the fields that apply are:

  • Your legal name, address and GSTIN; the client's name, address and GSTIN (for B2B)
  • A consecutive serial number unique to the financial year, no longer than 16 characters, using only letters, numbers, hyphen and slash (so AMS/26-27/014 works; "INV-final-2-revised" doesn't)
  • Invoice date, SAC code, description of services, taxable value
  • Tax shown separately: CGST 9% + SGST 9% (client in your state) or IGST 18% (client in another state)
  • Place of supply with the state name — this is what justifies the CGST/SGST-vs-IGST choice
  • Whether tax is payable on reverse charge, and a signature or digital signature — Rule 46 waives the signature for an electronic invoice issued under the IT Act, 2000, so an emailed PDF from your billing system doesn't need a scanned signature
  • For an unregistered client (a doctor, a small shop) and a taxable value of ₹50,000 or more: their name, address, and the state name and code

The 10-point check before you hit send

  1. Heading says "Tax Invoice"
  2. Your GSTIN, name and address
  3. Client's GSTIN (or state and code if unregistered and ₹50,000+)
  4. Serial number in sequence, 16 characters or fewer
  5. Invoice date within 30 days of the service period
  6. 6-digit SAC on every line
  7. Taxable value per line, ad spend excluded
  8. CGST + SGST or IGST — matches the place of supply
  9. Reverse charge: "No"
  10. Export? The LUT endorsement instead of a tax line

Timing rule agencies routinely miss: under Rule 47, a service invoice must be issued within 30 days of the supply of service. For a monthly retainer, that means invoicing month-served + 30 days at the latest — not batching a quarter's invoices in one guilty evening, which also wrecks the client's credit-matching and your own cash flow.

Routing ad spend through your invoice inflates your turnover (pulling registration and e-invoicing thresholds closer), makes you the float for someone else's ads, and muddies whether GST applies on money that was never your revenue.

Do you charge GST to clients outside India?

Genuine exports of services are zero-rated — if your client is outside India and pays you in convertible foreign exchange, you can invoice at 0% GST by filing a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) on the GST portal (a yearly, free filing). Without an LUT you'd pay IGST and claim a refund later, which is strictly worse for cash flow. For a remote-friendly agency this is the single highest-value GST fact: a US client retainer carries no GST at all, legally.

The export invoice looks different too. Rule 46 requires the endorsement "SUPPLY MEANT FOR EXPORT/SUPPLY TO SEZ UNIT OR SEZ DEVELOPER FOR AUTHORISED OPERATIONS UNDER BOND OR LETTER OF UNDERTAKING WITHOUT PAYMENT OF INTEGRATED TAX", plus the client's name and address and the country of destination in place of an Indian state. Copy that wording exactly; it is the line your refund or LUT trail hangs on.

How do TDS and e-invoicing fit in?

  • TDS: business clients deduct tax at source before paying (commonly 1–2% under Section 194C for advertising/contract work). Show GST as a separate line — TDS is then deducted on the taxable value, not on the GST component. Your ₹56,640 invoice arrives minus TDS on ₹48,000; reconcile against Form 26AS at filing. Which section and rate apply is its own rabbit hole — see our TDS on digital marketing services guide.
  • E-invoicing: mandatory only once turnover crosses ₹5 crore (threshold in force since 1 Aug 2023 — ClearTax). Most agencies are nowhere near it; just know the cliff exists so it doesn't surprise you in a good year. Two follow-ons once you cross it: from 1 April 2025, businesses with aggregate turnover of ₹10 crore or more can't report an e-invoice to the IRP more than 30 days after its date (GSTN advisory), and a business above the threshold that is exempt from e-invoicing must print the Rule 46 declaration saying so.
  • GSTR-1 discipline: your client's input credit shows up only after you report the invoice in your GSTR-1. File late and their accountant chases them, they chase you, and your next retainer conversation starts on the back foot.

FAQ

Is GST registration mandatory for a digital marketing agency below ₹20 lakh turnover?

No. Registration becomes mandatory only once aggregate turnover crosses ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special-category states), even if you bill clients in other states. Many small agencies register voluntarily anyway, because business clients prefer GST invoices they can claim input credit on, and you can claim credit on your own software spends.

Which SAC code applies to social media marketing services?

Use SAC 998361, advertising services, for social media management, SEO, content, influencer work and managing a client's Meta or Google ads. SAC 998365 is for selling internet advertising space or time yourself. Both attract 18% GST, so the tax doesn't change, but a consistent code keeps your client's matching clean.

Do I charge IGST or CGST + SGST to a digital marketing client?

It depends on the place of supply. If your registered client is in the same state as you, split the 18% into CGST 9% and SGST 9%. If the client is in another state, charge IGST 18%. Write the place of supply and state name on the invoice so the choice is visible.

How do I invoice a foreign client for digital marketing without GST?

File a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) on the GST portal each financial year, then invoice at 0% with the Rule 46 endorsement "supply meant for export under bond or letter of undertaking without payment of integrated tax", the client's address and the country. Payment must arrive in convertible foreign exchange, or rupees where RBI permits.

Can clients claim input tax credit on a digital marketing invoice?

Yes. For a GST-registered business client, marketing is a business expense, so the 18% you charge flows back to them as input tax credit, provided your invoice carries both GSTINs and the SAC code, and you report it in your GSTR-1 on time so it appears in their GSTR-2B.
