How Much to Pay UGC Creators in India (2026 Rates)

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published October 19, 2026

Most Indian UGC creators charge roughly ₹2,500 to ₹9,000 for one video used organically, depending on length and format; experienced creators ask ₹15,000 or more. If the brand wants to run that video as a paid ad, usage rights are billed on top, for a fixed period. Whitelisting and permanent buyouts cost more again.

A D2C founder once forwarded me a creator's quote with one line: "₹6,000 for a 30-second video? My editor costs less than that per week." Two weeks later the same founder wanted to put that video behind ₹2 lakh of Meta ad spend for three months, and was surprised the creator wanted more money for it.

Both reactions come from the same mistake. UGC is priced in two parts: making the content and the right to use it. Below: real Indian rate ranges, why usage rights are a separate line, and how an agency should quote UGC to clients.

What's the difference between a UGC creator and an influencer?

An influencer is paid for reach: their audience sees your product on their handle. A UGC creator is paid for content: a video that looks like a real customer made it, delivered to you as a file, to post on your own handle or run as an ad. Their follower count barely matters. Some of the best UGC creators we've hired had under 2,000 followers.

So don't price UGC off a follower-count rate card. If the creator also posts the video on their own handle, that's an influencer deal too, and the ASCI disclosure rules apply to that post.

How much do UGC creators charge in India?

There's no official survey of Indian UGC rates. Most published numbers come from marketplaces and agency blogs, so treat them as benchmarks, not rules. The clearest format-wise breakdown we found is Dazzlerr's June 2026 rate table for Indian creators (source), and it matches what we see when we hire for clients:

FormatTypical lengthIndian rate, per video (organic use)
Product unboxing30 sec₹2,500 – ₹4,500
Talking-head testimonial30 sec₹3,500 – ₹5,500
Lifestyle product demo60 sec₹5,000 – ₹7,500
Full tutorial / how-to90 sec₹6,500 – ₹9,000
Monthly retainer5–10 videos₹30,000 – ₹70,000 a month

Those are mid-market rates. In our experience, newer creators building a portfolio will take ₹1,500 to ₹3,000 a video, often plus the product. Creators with a proven record of ads that convert, or with a specialism such as skincare claims, finance explainers or fluent regional languages, charge ₹15,000 and up per video. As a rough global comparison, Influencers Time puts the median Indian video at about $175 against US and UK rates that "often start north of $300" (source).

What pushes a quote up

  • Script work. If the creator writes the hook and the script, that's extra work. If you send a tight brief, you can expect a lower quote.
  • Hook variations. Three different opening lines on the same video is how ads get tested. Expect ₹500 to ₹1,500 per extra hook.
  • Raw footage. Unedited clips let your editor make new cuts later. Creators price this separately, and they're right to.
  • Turnaround. Next-day delivery for a sale launch carries a rush premium, just like your own add-on price list does.
  • Exclusivity. "Don't make videos for any other skincare brand for 90 days" stops the creator earning from that category for three months, so it costs money.

Why should paid-ad usage rights be billed separately?

Because a video on your feed reaches people who already follow you. The same video as an ad might be shown to lakhs of new people for months, with the creator's face selling your product the whole time. The creator is entitled to share in that.

Rate-card guides describe the same ladder. Organic-only is the floor, paid-ad rights cost meaningfully more, and whitelisting (ads running from the creator's own handle) adds more again (Idukki). Influencers Time estimates usage fees at 50% to 150% of the base production fee depending on duration and channel, and a perpetual, cross-platform buyout at two to four times the original fee (source). Here's how that translates into a simple ladder you can quote from:

Usage tierWhat the brand getsTypical add-on to the base fee
Organic onlyPost on the brand's own handles, no boostingIncluded in the base fee
Paid ads, 3 monthsRun as ads from the brand's account on Meta and/or YouTube+50% to +100%
Paid ads, 6–12 monthsSame, longer window+100% to +150%
Whitelisting / partnership adsAds run from the creator's handle, with their name on topMonthly fee while the ads run, on top of the usage fee
Perpetual buyoutUse anywhere, forever, including offline2× to 4× the base fee in total

For whitelisting, a monthly fee works better than a one-time fee, because the creator's handle keeps working for you every month the ads run (Xolo's whitelisting guide argues the same). Meta now calls these partnership ads; the creator approves your access from their account.

My advice for small brands: buy organic plus a 3-month paid window up front, with a pre-agreed price to extend. Most UGC ads fatigue within weeks, so a perpetual buyout on a video that may never become an ad is wasted money. Winners get extended at a price you already know.

How should you structure the payment?

  1. Ship the product first, then pay. For a single video, 100% on delivery of the final file is normal. For retainers or high-end creators, 50% on brief sign-off and 50% on delivery is fair.
  2. Put everything in writing. Deliverables, revision rounds (one is standard), usage tier, usage period, exclusivity and the payment date. Our influencer contract template covers all of these clauses and works for UGC with small changes.
  3. Ask about GST. A creator whose turnover crosses the GST registration threshold (₹20 lakh for services in most states) will add 18% GST to the invoice. Budget for it.
  4. Ask your CA about TDS. TDS may apply to the fee depending on the amount and how the creator is engaged. Separately, free products worth more than ₹20,000 to one person in a year attract 10% TDS. That was Section 194R (ClearTax explainer), and from 1 April 2026 it sits in the consolidated TDS section of the new Income-tax Act, 2025.

How should an agency charge clients for UGC?

Agencies get this wrong in two ways: they bundle UGC into the retainer and absorb the cost, or they mark up the creator's fee by 40% without saying so. Both go wrong eventually. The clean model has two lines:

  • Creator fees at actuals, passed through with the creator's invoice attached, or paid by the client directly.
  • A coordination fee for the work the agency actually does: finding and vetting creators, writing the brief, managing revisions, the contract, the usage clause and the follow-ups. A flat fee per creator, or 15–20% of creator fees with a minimum, is common. Put it on your add-on price list so the client has seen it before the first project.

Your margin stays visible and defensible, and when the client asks "why did this video cost ₹12,000?", the answer is on the invoice. For retainer pricing itself, see how much to charge for social media management in India.

Write a brief that gets it right the first time

Most of the money lost on UGC goes on reshoots, and most reshoots come from a vague brief. Ours always fits on one page:

  1. The product and the one job the video must do (sell the feature, overcome a doubt, show the result).
  2. How the first two seconds should open, with two or three example hooks. Leave the rest in the creator's own voice.
  3. Specs: 9:16, length, on-screen captions yes or no, number of hook variations, raw files yes or no.
  4. Claims they must make, and claims they must not make. "Cures acne" can get your client in trouble.
  5. The usage tier and period, written exactly as it appears in the contract.

Then get the client's approval on the finished video before you pay the final instalment and before it goes into an ads account. A midnight "looks good" on WhatsApp is hard to find three months later, when someone asks who approved that claim.

Three mistakes that cost brands money

  • No usage clause at all. Then you're arguing about the price after the ad has already gone viral, which is the worst time to negotiate.
  • One video, one hook. The cheapest way to improve ad results is three hooks on one body. Order them up front, when they cost a few hundred rupees each.
  • Treating product as payment. A ₹1,200 serum is not payment for a ₹4,000 video. Barter works with beginners. With professionals, it costs you the good ones.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a UGC video cost in India?

For organic use on the brand's own handles, most Indian UGC creators charge about ₹2,500 to ₹9,000 per video, depending on format and length: unboxings at the low end, 90-second tutorials at the top. Beginners may accept ₹1,500 to ₹3,000, while experienced creators with proven ad results charge ₹15,000 or more.

Do I need to pay extra to run UGC as a Facebook or Instagram ad?

Yes, if the agreement only covers organic use. Paid-ad rights are usually priced as an add-on for a fixed window, commonly 50% to 150% of the base fee depending on how long and where the ad runs. Running ads from the creator's own handle, through Meta partnership ads, is normally a further monthly fee.

Is a perpetual buyout of UGC worth it?

Rarely, for small brands. A perpetual buyout often costs two to four times the base fee, and most UGC ads stop performing within weeks. Buy organic use plus a three-month paid window, and agree an extension price in the contract so you can keep a winning video running at a price you already know.

Should an agency mark up UGC creator fees?

It's cleaner to pass creator fees through at actual cost and charge a visible coordination fee for finding creators, briefing, revisions, contracts and usage tracking. A flat fee per creator or 15 to 20 percent of creator fees works. Hidden markups tend to come out eventually and damage the client's trust.
