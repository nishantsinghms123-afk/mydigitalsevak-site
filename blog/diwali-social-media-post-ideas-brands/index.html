Diwali Social Media Post Ideas for Brands: 2026 Plan

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published October 15, 2026

The best Diwali posts show the brand's own world: its product, team, store and customers, not a stock diya. Plan them as a countdown. Teasers start three weeks out, gift guides and offers run in the last ten days, Dhanteras gets a buying post, and the greeting goes out on Lakshmi Puja morning (Sunday 8 November 2026), scheduled in advance.

Every year, around 1 November, our WhatsApp fills up with the same message from clients: "Diwali ka kya plan hai?" And every year, the brands that get real results from Diwali are the ones whose plan was finished before that message arrived.

Diwali is the busiest week of the year for most Indian brands, and the noisiest week on Instagram. If your post looks like the 40 other diya-and-rangoli templates in someone's feed, it doesn't get seen. This guide covers what we actually post for our clients: the 2026 dates, a countdown plan, ideas by industry, formats that work, and the mistakes we've learned to avoid.

When is Diwali 2026? The dates that matter for posting

Lakshmi Puja is on Sunday 8 November 2026. Here is the five-day festival, using Drik Panchang's 2026 calendar for New Delhi, with what each day is for on social media:

DayDate (2026)What to post
DhanterasFri 6 NovBuying posts: gold, silver, utensils, vehicles, electronics, homes. The biggest sales day for many categories.
Naraka Chaturdashi / Choti DiwaliListed on 7 or 8 Nov, depending on the calendarLight content: decor, getting ready, last-minute gifting. In Tamil Nadu and much of the South, this morning is Deepavali.
Lakshmi Puja (Diwali)Sun 8 NovThe greeting post. No hard selling.
Govardhan PujaTue 10 Nov (Drik Panchang) · some calendars say 9 NovFood brands and regional brands. Gujarati New Year (Bestu Varas) falls around here too.
Bhai DoojWed 11 Nov (Drik Panchang) · some calendars say 10 NovSibling content and gifting. It works for fashion, sweets, jewellery and gift brands.
Calendars disagree by a day on the last three. Amavasya runs into 9 November this year, so some panchangs move Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj later. Before you schedule those posts, ask the client which date their family and customers will observe. It takes one WhatsApp message, and a greeting on the wrong day looks careless.

A countdown plan from now to Bhai Dooj

We plan Diwali backwards from the greeting. This is the schedule we use for a typical retail or D2C client:

WhenAgency workWhat goes live
15–22 OctLock the offer, shoot product and team content, design the greetingOne or two teasers ("something's coming")
23–31 OctCollect all approvals in one go and schedule everythingGift guides, behind-the-scenes, early-bird offers
1–5 NovAnswer DMs and comments quickly. This is when people buy.Offer reels with a clear end date, countdown stories, customer posts
6 NovWatch stock and the offer details. Fix things fast.Dhanteras buying post
8 NovNothing, if you planned well. The team should be home.Greeting post, scheduled for the morning
10–14 NovPull numbers for the client's reportGovardhan / Bhai Dooj post, a thank-you to customers and staff

Why start this early? Because people start shopping early. Meta's 2025 Digital Utsav festive playbook, made with Ipsos, found that 65% of festive shoppers engaged with Meta's apps, and that nearly half follow creators for buying guidance (Business Today). It's Meta's own research, so treat the numbers with some caution. But the pattern matches what we see: by Dhanteras, most people have already decided what to buy. A post on the day itself mostly reminds people who decided earlier.

What should brands post for Diwali? Ideas by industry

Generic ideas ("post a greeting, run a giveaway") don't help much. These are the posts that have done well for our clients, by category:

Jewellery

Dhanteras is your biggest day of the year. Post a reel on what to buy on Dhanteras at different budgets, explain making charges and hallmarking in plain words, and show real customers picking pieces in the store (with their permission). More in our guide to Instagram marketing for jewellery brands.

Restaurants, cafés and sweet shops

Show how the mithai is made: the kitchen at 5 am, the karigar's hands, the boxes being packed. Post the order cut-off date for corporate gift boxes clearly, and repeat it. See Instagram marketing for restaurants.

D2C fashion

Do one look for each of the five days, a "what to wear for Diwali puja" carousel, and Bhai Dooj gifting for brothers and sisters. Mention delivery cut-off dates for each city, because that is the question you'll get most in DMs. More in social media for D2C fashion brands.

Salons and spas

Diwali booking slots fill up fast, so post availability, not just offers ("only 6 slots left on 7 November"). Before-and-after festive looks work well. See social media for salons and spas.

Real estate and car dealers

Many families buy homes and vehicles on Dhanteras because it is considered auspicious. Post "book on Dhanteras, get possession or delivery on the day" stories with real families, and say how much paperwork can be done in advance. See real estate and car dealerships.

Doctors and clinics

Useful, not salesy: first aid for burns, advice for asthma patients during smoke and pollution, and diabetic-friendly sweet swaps. Keep the advice general and get the doctor to approve it. See social media for doctors and clinics.

CAs, B2B firms and NGOs

For trading clients, new account books (Chopda Pujan) and the new year matter more than the sale. A simple, classy greeting from the partners works better than a template. NGOs do well with a specific donation drive, such as "₹500 lights one family's Diwali", with a report afterwards. See our guides for CAs and lawyers and NGOs.

10 Diwali post formats that do better than a greeting card

  1. Gift guide carousel by budget: under ₹500, under ₹1,500, under ₹5,000. Easy to save and share.
  2. Team packing reel: staff packing orders or gift hampers. It's real, it's quick to shoot, and it shows you're busy.
  3. Founder's family Diwali: one short, honest reel. It works especially well for small brands where the founder is the face of the brand.
  4. Dhanteras buying guide: what to buy, why it's auspicious, and how your product fits.
  5. Customer rangoli or decor with your product: ask for photos and repost the best ones with credit.
  6. Countdown stories: days to Diwali, days left on the offer, and the delivery cut-off.
  7. Offer reel with a real end date: "Ends Thursday 5 Nov, midnight" beats "Limited time".
  8. Thank-you post for staff and delivery partners: name them and show their faces. People share these.
  9. Regional greeting: "Happy Deepavali" for Tamil clients, Kali Puja for Bengal, Bandi Chhor Divas for Sikh audiences. Use the word your customers use.
  10. Post-Diwali reset: detox recipes, clean-up tips, or a "back to routine" offer for the week after.

If the client has budget for a creator, a collab post with a local creator usually reaches further than the brand's own feed. Our guide to Instagram collab posts covers how to set one up.

What should brands avoid in Diwali posts?

  • Firecracker content. Rules on selling and using crackers change by state and city every year. In 2025, the Supreme Court allowed only green crackers in Delhi-NCR, sold for a few days from licensed places, with online sales still banned (Navbharat Live). Preaching against crackers is risky too: Tanishq faced a backlash in 2020 over an ad that did that (BOOM). For most brands, the safest choice is to show diyas and lights and leave crackers out.
  • Gods on discount banners. Putting Lakshmi or Ganesh next to "FLAT 50% OFF" offends people. Keep religious images to the greeting, and treat them with respect.
  • The same template as everyone else. If a free template site has it, 40 other brands are posting it on the same day.
  • Hard selling on Lakshmi Puja day. Post the greeting and stop. The offer can wait until the next morning.
  • Copyrighted film songs on brand reels. Festive songs are very tempting, but read our guide to music copyright on brand reels before you use one.

How do agencies run Diwali for 20 clients without panicking?

The work isn't hard. The problem is that it all lands in the same two weeks, and your team wants Diwali off too. What works for us:

  • One brief, many versions. Write a standard Diwali brief (offer, dates, products, delivery cut-off, regional greeting) and send it to every client on the same day in mid-October.
  • Batch the greetings. Design every client's greeting in one sprint and send them for approval together. Don't let approvals trickle in one by one until 7 November.
  • Schedule everything. Nobody on your team should be posting from their phone during Lakshmi Puja. See how to bulk-schedule a month of Instagram content and the best time to post in India.
  • Plan leave early. Decide in October who covers DMs and urgent changes on 6–7 November, and give everyone else 8 November off.

Frequently asked questions

When should brands start posting for Diwali?

Start teasers about three weeks before Lakshmi Puja and run gift guides and offers in the last ten days. For Diwali on 8 November 2026, that means teasers from mid-October and offers from around 28 October. The design and approvals should be finished before the first post goes live, not alongside it.

What should a brand's Diwali greeting post say?

Keep it short and specific to the brand. Show your own product, team, store or customers instead of a stock diya, and leave the sale out of the greeting itself. One line of warm copy plus your logo is enough. Post it on the morning of Lakshmi Puja, scheduled in advance, so nobody has to post from home.

Should brands post about firecrackers on Diwali?

Most brands should leave firecrackers out. Rules on sale and use change by state and city every year, and posts either for or against crackers can attract backlash. Show diyas, lights, family and food instead. If you do show crackers, show safe use outdoors with adults present, and never preach either way.

Is Diwali on 8 November in 2026?

Yes. Lakshmi Puja falls on Sunday 8 November 2026 and Dhanteras on Friday 6 November. Calendars differ by a day on Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj: Drik Panchang lists 10 and 11 November for New Delhi, while others list 9 and 10 November. Check the client's regional calendar before scheduling those posts.
