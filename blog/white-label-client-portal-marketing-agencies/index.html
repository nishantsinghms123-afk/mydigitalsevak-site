White Label Client Portal for Marketing Agencies

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published September 1, 2026

A white label client portal is software your agency rents and presents under its own brand — your logo, colours and often your domain — so the client experiences it as your product. Branding comes in levels, and only two of them change how a client feels. The rest is priced like it matters more than it does.

Search this term and you get eight vendor pages that all say the same sentence: put your logo on it and clients will think you built it. Nobody tells you which parts of white labelling clients actually notice, what the branding costs once you multiply it by your client count, or when spending on it is a mistake.

I have onboarded clients onto branded portals, watched them ignore those portals, and rebuilt the whole approach around what actually got opened. Here is the honest version.

What does "white label" actually mean on a client portal?

It is not one feature. It is five distinct layers, sold as one word, and vendors gate them at different price points. Knowing which layer a plan includes is the entire evaluation.

LevelWhat changesDoes the client notice?
1. Logo and coloursYour logo on the login screen and header, your brand colours through the interface, your favicon in the browser tabYes — this is the layer that carries almost all the perceived value
2. Product nameThe app is called "Acme Studio Hub" instead of the vendor's name, in the header, the page title and the home-screen icon labelYes — a vendor name in the title bar is the single biggest tell
3. Branded notificationsEmails and push notifications come from your name and your sending domain, not the vendor'sYes, but only when something breaks or lands in spam
4. Custom domainportal.youragency.com instead of youragency.vendor.comMostly no, on mobile. Yes for desktop-heavy or procurement-heavy clients
5. Full resale / "powered by" removalNo vendor attribution anywhere; you can present the platform as yours in contractsNo — this one is for your positioning, not their experience

Vendors document these unevenly. AgencyAnalytics describes its white labelling as your logo replacing theirs on the login screen and report headers, your icon as the favicon, your colours on dashboards, and reports served from your own domain — levels 1, 2 and 4. ManyRequests includes a custom domain on every plan but keeps "powered by" removal for its $99/month Pro tier and above — level 4 cheap, level 5 gated.

Which levels actually matter?

Levels 1 and 2. That is the finding, and it is unglamorous.

Here is why: an Indian client who uses a portal at all uses it on a phone. They open the link you sent on WhatsApp, and if the portal is a decent progressive web app they add it to their home screen. From that moment they see an icon with your logo and your name and they never see a URL again. The domain — the layer vendors charge the most for and market the hardest — has become invisible.

The exceptions are real but narrow. Custom domains earn their price when your clients are enterprises whose procurement or IT teams review every vendor in the stack, when the portal is used on desktop by a marketing team rather than on a phone by a founder, or when you are genuinely reselling the platform as your own product and the contract needs to say so.

For a five-to-thirty client Indian agency serving founders and marketing managers, none of those usually apply. Get levels 1 and 2 right, get level 3 right if you send report emails, and stop paying for the rest until a client asks.

What does a white label client portal cost?

The number that matters is not the sticker price, it is whether the vendor bills per client. That single design choice decides whether the cost is flat or grows with the thing you are trying to grow.

Pricing modelExampleCost at 10 clientsCost at 25 clients
Per client, per monthAgencyAnalytics — $20 per client/month billed annually, white labelling included$200/month$500/month
Per plan tierManyRequests — Core $59/mo, Pro $99/mo (branding removal), Enterprise from $1,000/mo$59–99/month$59–99/month, subject to plan limits

At ten clients the gap is roughly $100 a month. At twenty-five it is $400 a month, which is more than ₹35,000 — real money for an Indian agency, and it buys nothing extra as you scale. Add the forex markup your card issuer takes on every USD charge and the gap widens again.

So the first question to ask any portal vendor is not "do you white label" — almost all of them do. It is "what does my bill look like at three times my current client count". If the answer scales linearly with clients, you are renting your own growth back.

Watch for a second cost that nobody quotes: the portal that only holds one thing. A reporting-only portal at $20 a client is $20 a client for a monthly PDF your client could have received on WhatsApp. Branding does not change that arithmetic — it just makes the PDF prettier. The portal has to hold the calendar, the approvals and the invoices before per-client pricing is defensible. That is the argument in our piece on the all-in-one agency dashboard.

The five checks Indian agencies should run before buying

  1. Does it install to a phone home screen? If the client has to remember a URL and a password every time, the portal is dead in week three regardless of whose logo is on it. A progressive web app that installs in two taps is the difference between a portal and a bookmark.
  2. Can WhatsApp still be the doorbell? Telling an Indian client to stop WhatsApping you does not work. The portal should be where the answer lives, with WhatsApp remaining the nudge that sends them there — the demote-don't-replace rule we set out in WhatsApp vs client portal.
  3. Are approvals timestamped and attributable? A branded portal that captures "approved by Priya, 14 Aug, 4:12 PM" is worth ten times one that captures a thumbs-up. See what a client approval app should actually do.
  4. Can a GST invoice live in the same portal? Most global portal tools stop at reporting and hand billing to a separate tool. If your client has to look in two branded places for two answers, you have half a portal. GST specifics are in our GST invoicing guide.
  5. Is the price in rupees? A USD subscription re-prices itself every time the rupee moves, and you cannot pass that on to a client on a fixed annual retainer.

When white labelling is the wrong thing to buy

Three situations where I would tell an agency to skip it entirely:

  • Under five clients. At that size your clients are talking to you personally every week. The portal is solving a problem you do not have yet, and the money is better spent on the person who makes the content.
  • When the portal has nothing to show. Branding an empty room does not furnish it. If your calendar is still in a spreadsheet and approvals are still in a WhatsApp thread, a portal will be logged into once and abandoned. Fix the workflow, then brand it.
  • When you are white labelling to hide the tool rather than to look consistent. Clients are not impressed by the idea that you built the software; most assume you did not, and the ones who ask are fine with the truth. The honest reason to white label is that your client should not have to learn a third-party brand to work with you — not that you want credit for building a product.

The related trap is migration cost. Moving twenty clients from a portal they finally learned to a new branded one costs you goodwill you cannot invoice for. Pick the branding layer you need at the client count you expect in eighteen months, not the one you need this quarter — that stability is quietly one of the better levers on client churn.

Frequently asked questions

What is a white label client portal?

A white label client portal is software your agency rents but presents under its own identity — your logo, your colours and often your domain — so clients experience it as your product rather than a third-party tool. The underlying platform is still the vendor's; only the surface your client sees is yours.

Does a white label client portal need a custom domain?

Rarely, if your clients are on mobile. A client who installs the portal to their phone home screen sees your icon and your app name and almost never sees a URL again. Custom domains matter most when clients open the portal on desktop, when procurement reviews vendors, or when you are reselling the platform.

How much does a white label client portal cost?

It depends entirely on whether the vendor charges per client. AgencyAnalytics lists a single plan at 20 US dollars per client per month billed annually with white labelling included, so ten clients is 200 dollars a month. ManyRequests charges by plan instead, from 59 dollars a month, with branding removal only from the 99 dollar tier.

Will clients actually use a white labelled portal?

Only if the portal answers a question they already have — what is going live this week, what needs my approval, what did last month deliver. Branding does not create usage. In India, expect to keep WhatsApp as the notification layer and use the portal as the place the answer lives, not as a replacement for chat.
