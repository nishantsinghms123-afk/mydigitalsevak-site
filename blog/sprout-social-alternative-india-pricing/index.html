Sprout Social Alternative for India: Pricing in Rupees (2026)

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published September 7, 2026

Sprout Social has no India price list. Its cheapest agency-usable plan is $199 per seat per month, so a four-person team pays about ₹7 lakh a year before 18% GST under reverse charge. That is more than many Indian agencies bill a client in a year, which is why the alternative you pick should be chosen on seat maths first and features second.

Every "Sprout Social alternatives" list on the first page of Google is written by a competing tool, and every one of them quotes dollar prices to readers who pay salaries in rupees. I want to do the sum the other way round: start with what an Indian agency actually earns per client, and see what Sprout's per-seat model does to it. Then pick alternatives on the basis of which of Sprout's jobs you were really buying. If you are here from the Hootsuite post, the reverse-charge logic is the same, but the seat multiplier makes Sprout the harder case.

Everything below is from Sprout's own pricing page as of this week. The per-seat figures are the ones Sprout displays for annual billing; pricing trackers such as StackScored and eesel report the same numbers, with monthly billing running higher. Check the page before you budget, because Sprout has added a tier in the last year and may again.

Two things in that table decide everything for an agency. First, every plan is priced per seat, and the seat is a person, not a client. Second, Essentials and Standard cap you at five social profiles. An agency with three clients on Instagram and Facebook is already at six profiles, so the realistic entry plan for multi-client work is Professional at $299 a seat. Listening, Premium Analytics and Employee Advocacy are paid add-ons on top.

What does that mean in rupees?

Take a four-person agency: a founder, an account manager, a designer and an editor. Everyone touches the calendar, so everyone needs a seat. I have used ₹88 to the dollar as a round figure; substitute the day's rate and the shape does not change.

Now add tax. A foreign SaaS subscription is an OIDAR service for GST purposes. If your agency is GST-registered, you self-assess 18% IGST under reverse charge; IndiaFilings has a clear explainer. You can normally take that back as input credit, so for a registered agency it is a cash-flow cost, not a permanent one, but it still goes out of the bank first. If you are a freelancer or a small shop that is not registered, the 18% is simply added and gone. We covered the mechanics in the GST invoicing post.

Put that against what Indian agencies actually charge. In the pricing survey we ran on retainers, most small-city retainers sit between ₹15,000 and ₹50,000 a month. The two-seat Professional setup above costs the equivalent of one to three full retainers every month, before you have paid a single salary. In Mumbai or Bengaluru, where retainers run higher, the sum is less brutal but still means Sprout is the third or fourth biggest line in the P&L.

Why do agencies still pay it?

Because Sprout is genuinely excellent at things most Indian agencies do not sell. Its listening product, the unified inbox with tagging and assignment, review management and the reporting depth are built for in-house teams at large brands with a community manager on shift. If your client is a bank or a telecom and you are the extended community team, Sprout's inbox earns its price and I would not argue with it.

The mismatch is that most agencies searching for an alternative are not that. They are content agencies: plan a month, get it approved, publish it, report on it, invoice for it. Of Sprout's feature list, that workflow uses publishing and basic reporting. It does not use listening, sentiment or the helpdesk integrations. And the one thing that workflow badly needs, a client-facing approval step and an invoice at month end, Sprout does not do at all. Sprout's approval workflow is internal to your team; there is no client login. Billing is not in the product.

How do you switch without losing the month?

  1. Export the calendar first. Sprout lets you export scheduled posts and reports. Pull the next 30 days of scheduled content and the last three months of reports before your renewal date, not after.
  2. Reconnect profiles through the official API. Whatever you move to should connect Instagram and Facebook via Meta's API with the client as admin, never through shared passwords. The reasons are in the password-sharing post.
  3. Run one client in parallel for two weeks. Schedule the same posts in both tools for one low-stakes client. If the new tool's approval loop and publishing hold up, move the rest.
  4. Cancel at the renewal boundary. Sprout's annual pricing is a commitment. Set the reminder for 30 days before, because a missed renewal on a four-seat Professional plan is a ₹12 lakh mistake.
  5. Tell the clients. A new approval portal is a client-facing change. One WhatsApp message with a screenshot of the new "approve" button prevents a month of "where do I click".

The test I keep coming back to, and the one we built the agency management software page around: count the seats you are paying for and count the clients you are billing. If the first number is growing faster than the second, the tool is priced for a brand's in-house team, not for you.
