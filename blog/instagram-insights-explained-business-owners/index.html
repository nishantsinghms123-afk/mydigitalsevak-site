Instagram Insights Explained for Business Owners (2026)

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published 26 Nov 2026

Instagram Insights shows how your content performs. Views count every time a post, reel or story is shown, including repeats. Reach counts the unique accounts that saw it. Interactions are likes, comments, saves, shares and replies. For a business, the useful numbers are reach, saves and shares, profile visits and link taps, and follows.

Every month, at least one client sends us a screenshot of their Insights with a question mark. "Views 48K, reach 9K. Which one is real?" "Why did impressions disappear?" "My nephew says my engagement is bad. Is it?"

They're fair questions. Instagram changed its main metric recently, third-party tools calculate things differently, and none of the screens explain what a number means for a restaurant or a clinic. Here is the plain-English version we give our own clients, and the four numbers we tell them to actually watch.

Where do you find Instagram Insights?

Insights is only available on a professional account (Business or Creator). On a personal account, switch in Settings first. Then:

  • Account level: open your profile, tap Professional dashboard, then Account insights. Pick the date range at the top.
  • Post level: open any post or reel and tap View insights under it.

Insights only count from the day you switched to a professional account. If you switched last month, there's no history before that.

What do views, reach and interactions mean?

Here is every number you'll see, in the order a business owner should care about it:

MetricWhat it countsWhat it tells you
ReachUnique accounts that saw your content at least onceHow many people you got in front of. The base for most other numbers.
ViewsEvery time your content was shown or played, repeats includedTotal attention. Views well above reach means people rewatched.
InteractionsLikes, comments, saves, shares and repliesWhether people did something, not just scrolled.
Accounts engagedUnique accounts that interacted at least onceHow many real people engaged, not how many taps.
Saves and sharesPeople keeping the post or sending it onThe strongest signals that the content was useful.
Profile visits and link tapsPeople who went to your profile or tapped your website, call or directions buttonsThe step before an enquiry. Closest number to business.
FollowsAccounts that followed you from a piece of contentWhether a post brought new audience, not just views.
Skip rate (reels only)Share of viewers who scrolled past within the first 3 secondsWhether your opening worked. Lower is better.

Each of these can be split into followers and non-followers. That split matters more than most people think: a reel with 80% non-follower reach is finding new customers; a post seen almost only by followers is talking to people who already know you.

Why did impressions disappear?

Instagram used to show "impressions" for posts and "plays" for reels. It replaced both with a single metric, views, which works the same way across reels, photos, carousels and stories. Meta also removed impressions from its API on 21 April 2025, according to Sprout Social's notice to its users, so every reporting tool now shows views instead.

What this means in practice: don't compare this year's views with last year's impressions. They're counted differently. If a report from 2024 says 30,000 impressions and this month's says 45,000 views, that is not proof of 50% growth. Compare reach with reach, which didn't change.

What is skip rate?

Skip rate is a newer reel metric: the share of people who scrolled past your reel in its first three seconds. Metricool's guide describes it as replacing the older view-rate figure, and lower is better. There's no official benchmark, so compare each reel against your own recent reels. If one reel has a much higher skip rate, the first shot or first line is the problem, not the rest of the video. Our reel hooks guide has openings that hold attention.

Which numbers should a business owner actually watch?

Ignore likes as a headline. They're the cheapest action on Instagram and the least connected to sales. These four are enough for a monthly check:

  1. Reach, and how much of it is non-followers. Is Instagram showing you to new people?
  2. Saves and shares. Is the content worth keeping or sending to a friend?
  3. Profile visits and link taps. Are people taking the step towards contacting you?
  4. Follows from content. Is the account growing because of what you post?

Which one matters most depends on the business. For a restaurant or salon, it's taps on directions and call. For a D2C brand, it's website taps and shares. For a clinic, coaching institute or real estate agent, it's profile visits followed by DMs and calls. We go through this in more detail in how to prove social media ROI.

If reach is growing but profile visits aren't, the content is entertaining people without making them curious about you. That's a content problem, not an algorithm problem.

Five misreadings we correct every month

  • "Views went up, so we're growing." If reach is flat, the same people are rewatching. Nice, but not growth.
  • "My engagement rate is 2%, is that bad?" Ask what it's divided by. Some tools divide interactions by reach, some by views, some by followers. The same month can show three different rates. Pick one formula and stick to it.
  • "This reel flopped compared to last week's." Compare reels with reels and statics with statics. Reels usually reach more people; that doesn't make every static a failure.
  • "Reach dropped this week." Check the calendar first. Fewer posts, a festival week, or one viral reel last week explains most drops. If it's more than that, our guide to why reach drops on business accounts has the full checklist.
  • "Insights says 500 people visited my profile but nobody called." Look at the profile they landed on. A bio without a phone number, WhatsApp link or location loses those visits.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between views and reach on Instagram?

Views count every time your content is shown or played, including repeats by the same person. Reach counts unique accounts that saw it at least once. If one customer watches your reel three times, that is three views and one account reached. Views higher than reach usually means people are rewatching.

Where did impressions go on Instagram?

Instagram replaced impressions and plays with a single metric called views, which works the same way across reels, posts and stories. Meta also removed impressions from its API on 21 April 2025, so reports from scheduling and reporting tools now show views instead. Old impression numbers are not directly comparable with views.

What is a good skip rate for Instagram reels?

Skip rate is the share of viewers who scrolled past your reel within the first three seconds, so lower is better. There is no official benchmark. Compare each reel against your own recent reels: if one has a clearly higher skip rate, the opening shot or first line is the problem, not the rest of the video.
