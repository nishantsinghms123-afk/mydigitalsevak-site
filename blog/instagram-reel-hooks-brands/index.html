Instagram Reel Hooks for Brands: 9 Formulas by Industry

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published October 24, 2026

A Reel hook for a brand is what the viewer sees, reads and hears in the first three seconds. Instagram reports a Skip Rate for exactly that window. The hooks that work for brands open on the product, result or problem rather than a logo, match a line of on-screen text to the visual, and pay off within the Reel.

Most brand Reels don't fail because the edit is bad. They fail at second one, when the clip opens on a logo animation, a slow pan across a shop counter, or someone saying "Hi everyone, welcome to our page." By the time the useful part starts, the viewer has already scrolled past.

We script Reels every week for restaurants, jewellers, clinics, real estate developers, gyms and D2C labels. This is the hook playbook our team works from: the formulas, examples by industry, and how we test them without throwing away a month's calendar.

Why do the first three seconds of a Reel matter so much?

Because Instagram measures them separately now. In August 2025 Instagram added a Retention chart to Reel insights and replaced "View Rate" with "Skip Rate", which it defines as "the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds." That gives you a number for every Reel that tells you how well its opening held people. Instagram's own guidance on the Retention chart is that you'll always see a downward slope, "but the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is."

This isn't new behaviour, only a new metric. Back in 2017 Facebook told advertisers that, by its research, 47% of a video's value is delivered in the first three seconds and 74% in the first ten. A brand Reel is judged in feed in the same way an ad is: people decide almost instantly whether it's worth their time.

Instagram doesn't publish a "good" skip rate, and you should ignore anyone who quotes one as official. We compare each Reel only against the same account's recent Reels.

What are the three layers of a Reel hook?

When a client says "the hook," they usually mean the first spoken line. That's only one of three layers, and in a feed where a lot of people watch with the sound off, it often isn't the most important one.

  1. The visual hook (frame one). What's on screen before anything is said: molten gold being poured, a cracked heel before a pedicure, a plot of land with no building on it. If frame one is static or empty, the other two layers have to work twice as hard.
  2. The text hook. A single line of on-screen text, readable in under two seconds, kept clear of the caption and buttons at the bottom of the screen. It should add to the visual, not repeat it.
  3. The spoken hook. One short sentence, or a sound cue. It should start straight away, not after a breath, a smile and a "so".

The best hooks use all three together: an interesting frame, text that tells you why to keep watching, and a voice that confirms it.

Which hook formulas work best for brand Reels?

These are the nine formulas our team uses most, roughly in order of how often they get used across our client roster:

One rule applies to every row: the Reel has to deliver what the hook promises. A clickbait opening may lower the skip rate for one Reel, but viewers who feel tricked drop off halfway through and stop trusting the account. That shows up in the Retention chart as a steep fall right after the hook.

What hooks work for each industry?

The formulas stay the same, but which one works best depends heavily on what the brand sells. Here's what we open with, by category.

Restaurants and cafés

The food has to be moving in frame one: a cheese pull, tadka hitting the dal, a dosa being folded. Text hook: "The ₹180 breakfast people queue for at 8 am." Price and process are the two formulas that consistently hold for food. More in our guide to Instagram marketing for restaurants in India.

Jewellery

Close-up, in natural light, moving: a hand turning, a clasp closing, stones catching the light. Text hook: "How to tell real polki from a copy in 5 seconds." Process and named-mistake hooks also build trust, which matters a lot in a category full of imitators. See our jewellery brand playbook.

D2C fashion

Open on the transformation, not the flat lay: one outfit, three ways, cut on the beat. Text hook: "1 kurta, 3 occasions, ₹1,499." For sizing questions, a customer's-own-words hook works well because it answers the viewer's main worry straight away. More in social media for D2C fashion brands.

Doctors and clinics

Use the myth-vs-fact and direct-question formulas, delivered by the doctor to camera. Text hook: "Knee pain on the stairs? Here's what it usually means." Steer clear of before/after results and promises of outcomes. Medical advertising has stricter rules, and our guide for doctors and clinics covers what to stay away from.

Real estate

Start with the view from the balcony or the walk in through the door, not the building's exterior at dusk. Text hook: "What ₹85 lakh gets you in Sector 150." A specific price is the strongest hook in property, and a hook that names a locality pulls in local buyers. More in our real estate guide.

Gyms and fitness studios

A member mid-movement, with the trainer correcting their form. Text hook: "You're doing squats wrong if your heels lift." Named-mistake hooks are the most reliable formula for fitness, because the viewer checks themselves against them. More in our gym marketing guide.

How do you plan hooks before the shoot?

The hook should be decided in the brief, not found in the edit. If the first frame wasn't shot on purpose, the editor can't create it later. In every Reel brief, our creators get three lines above the script: the frame-one shot, the text hook and the spoken hook, plus one alternate text hook to test.

That brief lives in the same calendar entry the client approves. When the client signs off on the hook before the shoot, we get far fewer "can we change the opening?" revisions after the edit. (We wrote more about that in handling client revisions.) If your team uses AI for first drafts, ask it for ten text hooks per Reel and pick from those. Don't publish the first one it gives you. Our AI caption guide has the prompting approach.

How should you test which hook works?

  1. Film two openings for every Reel. They only cost a few extra seconds of footage on shoot day. Edit the same body with each.
  2. Use Trial Reels for the alternate. Trial Reels are shown to non-followers first, so you can test an opening without filling your followers' feed with near-duplicates. Our Trial Reels playbook explains the setup.
  3. Compare skip rate and the Retention chart after a few days. Skip rate tells you which opening held people. The Retention chart tells you whether the payoff kept them.
  4. Log the winner by formula, not just by Reel. After a quarter you'll know that "price reveal" works for one café and "process" for another. That knowledge is worth more than any single viral Reel.
  5. Schedule the winner properly. Post when the audience is online. See our best time to post in India guide, and use an Instagram scheduler so a strong hook doesn't go out at 2 am because someone forgot.

Which hook mistakes kill brand Reels?

  • Logo or intro card first. Put the brand inside the frame, on the packaging, the apron or the signboard, instead of in front of it.
  • The "welcome back" opening. Greetings take up the most valuable second of the Reel.
  • Text in the danger zone. Text placed low on screen gets covered by the caption and buttons. Check it on a real phone before publishing.
  • A hook that needs sound. If the joke only works with audio, a large share of viewers never gets it. Put the punchline in the text as well.
  • Trending audio the brand can't use. Business accounts face music restrictions. Read our note on copyright music in Reels before building a hook around a song.
  • The same formula every day. Even a winning hook gets tired. Rotate between two or three formulas per client across the month.

None of this needs a big production budget. It's a planning habit. The same approach works for a café in Dehradun and a developer in Delhi NCR: decide frame one before you shoot, put the key point in the text, and check the skip rate before you repeat a formula.

FAQ

How long should an Instagram Reel hook be?

Treat the first three seconds as the hook, because that is the window Instagram's Skip Rate measures. In practice the visual has to land in the first frame, the on-screen text should be readable in under two seconds, and the spoken line should be one short sentence. Anything longer is a setup, not a hook.

What is a good skip rate for Instagram Reels?

Instagram has not published an official benchmark, so be wary of anyone quoting one. Compare each Reel against the same account's own recent Reels instead. If a Reel's skip rate is clearly worse than the account's usual range, change the opening frame and text first, before you touch the rest of the edit.

Should a brand Reel start with the logo?

No. A logo or intro card in the first second gives the viewer nothing to stay for, and early research for Facebook found much of a video's value is delivered in the first three seconds. Show the product, the result or the person first, and place the brand in the frame or the text instead.

Do hooks need to be in Hindi or English for Indian brands?

Use the language your client's customers speak in their own comments and DMs. Many of our north Indian brands get better holds with Hinglish voiceovers and English on-screen text, but that is our experience, not a rule. Test one Reel in each and keep the one with the lower skip rate.
