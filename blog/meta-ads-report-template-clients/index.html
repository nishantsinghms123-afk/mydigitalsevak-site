The Meta Ads Report Template Clients Actually Read (One Page)

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published 21 Nov 2026

A Meta Ads client report should fit one page: spend, results and cost per result vs last period; the money metric that matches the objective (ROAS, cost per lead or cost per conversation); a short campaign table; one or two breakdowns that changed a decision; the best and worst ad; and what worked, what didn't and what's next.

Search "Meta Ads report template" and you get reporting-tool galleries: dashboards with twenty widgets, built to show off the tool. Some are genuinely useful. Whatagraph, for instance, publishes separate lead-gen and e-commerce versions, which is the right instinct. But nearly all of them assume a marketing manager who reads dashboards.

Our ad clients are mostly owners: a jeweller, a clinic, a real-estate developer, a D2C founder. They open the report on a phone, usually from WhatsApp, and read three things: how much they spent, what they got, and whether we're doing something about what isn't working. Everything else is for us. So the template below is built backwards from that owner.

What should a Meta Ads report for clients include?

Eight blocks, in this order. Copy the structure; the example lines show the tone.

#SectionWhat goes in itExample line
1HeaderBrand, ad account, date range, comparison period, attribution setting, and whether spend includes GST"1–31 Oct vs 1–30 Sep · 7-day click, 1-day view · spend excludes GST"
2Four headline numbersSpend, results, cost per result, and the objective's money metric, each with the change vs last period"₹62,400 spent · 214 leads · ₹292 per lead (was ₹341)"
3OverviewTwo or three sentences. Write it last, put it first"Leads up, cost per lead down 14%. The new reel ad did most of the work."
4Campaign tableUp to five rows: campaign, objective, spend, results, cost per result, and one verdict word"Diwali catalogue · Sales · ₹28,000 · 41 orders · ₹683 · Scale"
5BreakdownsOnly the one or two (age, gender, region, placement, device) that changed a decision"Reels placement cost per lead was half of Feed, so budget moved to Reels."
6Best and worst adThumbnail, the numbers, one line on why"Price-in-first-frame reel: ₹190 per lead. Lifestyle static: ₹610, paused."
7What worked / what didn'tThree bullets each, plain language, every bullet with a numberSee below
8Next monthBudget split, what you'll test, what you need from the client"Test a ₹999 entry offer. Need: leads called back within 2 hours."

If a section has nothing useful to say this month, cut it. The page limit is the discipline.

Which number goes on top? It depends on the objective

Meta's "cost per result" is simply amount spent divided by results, and a "result" means whatever the campaign was optimised for. That's why one generic template fails: a "result" for a sales campaign and for a messaging campaign are different animals. Pick the headline by objective:

ObjectiveHeadline metricSupportingLeave out
Sales (website / catalogue)Revenue and ROASOrders, average order value, cost per orderCPM, link CTR
Leads (instant form or site)Leads and cost per leadHow many were genuine, from the client's own follow-upLink clicks
Messages (WhatsApp, Instagram DM, Messenger)Conversations started and cost per conversationHow many turned into enquiries or bookingsReach
Awareness / reachReach and frequencyCPM, video viewsCost per result in isolation
TrafficLanding page views and cost per viewTime on site from the client's analyticsLink clicks without page views

The messages row matters more in India than the global templates admit. For a lot of local businesses, the best ad sends people straight into a WhatsApp chat, and "₹38 per conversation" is a number an owner understands instantly. If you're running click-to-WhatsApp ads, report conversations, not clicks. (More on wiring WhatsApp into agency work in our WhatsApp Business API guide.)

Why don't the client's numbers match Ads Manager?

This is the argument that kills trust, so pre-empt it in the header. Three causes cover nearly every case:

  • Attribution windows. Meta credits a purchase to an ad clicked or viewed within a window, so its order count rarely equals the client's store dashboard. The windows also changed this year: from 12 January 2026, Meta stopped returning 7-day view and 28-day view data in the Ads Insights API (Meta's developer announcement). If last year's reports used 28-day view, this year's will look worse for no real reason. Say which window you use, every time.
  • GST on spend. Ads Manager shows spend before tax; Meta's invoice to an Indian advertiser adds GST on top. The owner compares your number with the card statement and sees a gap. Write "spend excludes GST" in the header and the question never comes.
  • Double counting. Meta and Google can both claim the same order. Where you can, put the client's own order or lead count next to Meta's and say which one the plan is judged on.

How do you write "what worked / what didn't" in plain words?

This is the section owners actually read, and the one most templates leave as an empty text box. Rules we follow:

  • Name the thing. Not "creative performance improved" but "the reel with the price in the first second".
  • Every bullet carries a number. "Women 25–34 in Gujarat brought 46% of orders for 31% of spend."
  • Every "didn't" carries an action. "Audience Network placements: 0 orders on ₹4,100. Switched off on 18 Oct."
  • Admit the client-side problems kindly. "62 leads weren't called back within a day. Fast callbacks are where the next drop in cost per lead will come from."

Weak version: "CTR was 1.8% and CPM increased due to festive competition." Strong version: "Diwali week cost more to reach people, as it does every year, but orders held up because the catalogue ads kept converting. We'll pull budget back to normal from 5 Nov." (The figures in these examples are illustrations, not benchmarks.) One sentence an owner can repeat to their partner beats a chart they can't read.

Breakdowns work the same way. Region is the one that most often changes decisions for our brands. A jewellery client in Jaipur may find buyers outside Rajasthan cost more but order bigger. Show the breakdown only when it moved budget; otherwise it goes in an appendix nobody opens. Our guide to Instagram marketing for jewellery brands goes deeper on that category.

When and how should you send it?

Monthly, on the 1st, as one PDF or link that opens cleanly on a phone, sent together with the organic social report and the invoice. If you run organic too, the monthly social media report covers the other half; keep the two separate but send them together. For heavy spenders or a live sale, add a two-line weekly WhatsApp update: spend so far, results, cost per result, one action. Weekly full reports get ignored.

Frequently asked questions

What should a Meta Ads report for clients include?

Spend, results and cost per result against the previous period; the one money metric that matches the objective, such as ROAS, cost per lead or cost per conversation; a short campaign table; one or two breakdowns that changed a decision; the best and worst ad; what worked and what didn't in plain words; and next month's plan.

How often should an agency send Meta Ads reports to clients?

Monthly for the full one-page report, sent on the 1st alongside the invoice and the social report. For clients spending heavily or running a sale, add a two-line weekly update on WhatsApp: spend so far, results, cost per result, and one action. Weekly full reports mostly get ignored.

Why don't Meta Ads numbers match the client's own sales figures?

Meta counts conversions inside an attribution window, so a sale can be credited to an ad clicked days earlier, and two platforms can claim the same order. Spend in Ads Manager also excludes the GST on Meta's invoice. State the attribution setting and GST treatment on the report so nobody argues about the gap.
