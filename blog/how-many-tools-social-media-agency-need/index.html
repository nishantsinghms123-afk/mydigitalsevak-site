How Many Tools Does a Social Media Agency Need? (2026 Costs)

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published November 21, 2026

A social media agency has about ten jobs that need software: plan, design, approve, schedule, tasks, chat, time, invoicing, reports and CRM. Bought one tool per job, that's five to nine subscriptions, about ₹8,100–₹36,800 a month for a 5-person, 10-client Indian agency at today's list prices. Consolidated, you need three plus WhatsApp.

Nobody plans a ten-tool stack. It grows one card swipe at a time. You start with Canva and a Google Sheet. A client complains about missed posts, so you add a scheduler. A designer misses a deadline, so you add a task board. Two years later the founder is paying nine renewals on three cards, half in dollars, and nobody remembers who has the Toggl login.

We went through exactly this at our agency before we built our own software, so I'll be upfront: I'm biased toward consolidation. But the numbers below are not mine. They are each vendor's own list price, checked on 21 November 2026, so you can redo the sums for your agency.

What jobs does a social media agency need software for?

Strip away the brand names and every agency does the same ten jobs. The question isn't how many tools you need. It's how many of these jobs you need separate tools for.

  1. Plan — the monthly content calendar per brand.
  2. Design — statics, carousels, reel covers, edits.
  3. Approve — getting the client to say yes, in writing.
  4. Schedule — publishing to Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube.
  5. Tasks — who's making what, by when.
  6. Chat — internal team conversation.
  7. Time — hours per client, if you bill or price by effort.
  8. Invoice — GST invoices, TDS, payment follow-ups.
  9. Report — the monthly performance report each client expects.
  10. CRM — leads, proposals and follow-ups for new business.

Three things stand out. First, reporting is the biggest line, because per-client tools grow with every brand you sign. Second, more than 80% of the full stack's cost is billed in dollars, so your software bill moves with the exchange rate. Third, the "lean" column has a hidden cost: the reports and time tracking it skips are done by hand, usually by the founder, in the first week of every month.

What's the cost that isn't on the invoice?

Subscriptions are the visible cost. The bigger one is the copying between tools. A caption is written in the sheet, pasted into the scheduler, pasted again into the report. A post approved on WhatsApp has to be marked approved on the board by someone who remembers. An invoice amount is retyped from the scope document. Every copy is a chance to get it wrong, and every tool is one more login to create when someone joins and one more to revoke when they leave.

We made the case for counting those hand-offs in our piece on the all-in-one agency dashboard, and the margin impact in social media agency profit margins in India. The short version: price the hours, not just the subscriptions.

  • Google Workspace. You need email anyway, and Drive is where your final files live. Our publishing pulls media straight from the brand's Drive folder.
  • WhatsApp. Clients will message you there forever. The goal is to stop running approvals and task chasing inside chat threads, not to leave WhatsApp. WhatsApp reminders also need your own WhatsApp Business provider connected, which is a separate account.

Add one more client or one more person and you move up a plan, so check the limits against your own roster.

How many tools is right for your agency's size?

  • Solo or 1–3 clients: three tools. Canva, Google Workspace and Meta Business Suite (free) for scheduling. Don't buy a stack for problems you don't have yet. Our guide to free scheduling tools covers where the free options run out.
  • 3–10 clients, 2–5 people: this is where approvals and deadlines start breaking. Either the lean stack above, or a platform plus Canva and Workspace.
  • 10–30 clients: per-client pricing starts to hurt, and the copying tax is now a full-time job. Consolidate, or hire someone whose job is operations.

One rule works at every size: add a tool only when you can name the specific, recurring pain it removes, and remove one whenever two tools hold the same information.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum tool stack for a new social media agency?

Three tools and WhatsApp: Canva for design, Google Workspace for email, Drive and a calendar sheet, and Meta Business Suite for scheduling Instagram and Facebook, which is free. Add paid tools only when a specific pain appears, such as missed deadlines, slow approvals or reports that take days to assemble.

How much does a social media agency spend on software in India?

Using list prices we checked on 21 November 2026, a 5-person agency with 10 clients spends roughly ₹8,100 a month on a lean stack and about ₹36,800 on a full one with paid reporting, time tracking, Slack and a CRM. Per-client reporting tools are usually the largest single line.

Can one platform replace all social media agency tools?

Not all of them. An agency platform can replace the calendar, approvals, scheduling, tasks, team chat, time tracking, invoicing, reports and lead tracking. You will still keep Canva for design, Google Workspace for email and Drive, WhatsApp for client conversations, and your accountant's own software.

Which agency tool costs grow fastest as you add clients?

Tools priced per client, per brand or per channel. Reporting tools such as AgencyAnalytics charge per client, and schedulers such as Buffer charge per channel, so every new client raises the bill. Per-user tools grow with headcount instead, which usually rises more slowly than the client list.
