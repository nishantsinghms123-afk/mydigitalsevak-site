Proforma Invoice vs Tax Invoice: A GST Guide for Agencies

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published 28 Nov 2026

A proforma invoice is a quote: it asks for payment or approval, creates no GST liability and gives the client no input credit. A tax invoice is the GST document that records your service. Send a proforma before the work is confirmed; issue the tax invoice once you supply the service or receive payment, whichever comes first.

Most agencies meet the proforma the same way. A new client, usually a school, a hospital or a company with a purchase department, says: "Send us a proforma, we'll raise the PO and release the advance." So you make one. The client pays two days later. And then nobody is quite sure what happens next. Was GST due when you sent the proforma? When they paid? When you started work? Do you send another invoice?

This guide answers those questions for a service business like a social media or digital marketing agency. It is not tax advice for your specific case; your CA should confirm anything unusual. But the basic rules are clear, and most agencies get one of them wrong.

What is the difference between a proforma invoice and a tax invoice?

The short version: a proforma says "this is what you will owe". A tax invoice says "this is what you owe, and here is the GST on it". Side by side:

Indian accounting and payments companies explain it the same way: ClearTax and Tally both describe the proforma as a pre-sale document that doesn't count for returns or input credit. When should an agency send a proforma invoice?

Only when the client needs a document before they can commit. In our experience that's a short list:

  • Purchase orders. Schools, hospitals, manufacturers and most companies above a certain size need a proforma to raise a PO or get internal approval.
  • Advance before the first month. A new client pays the first retainer upfront, and you don't want to issue a tax invoice for work that might not start.
  • One-off projects. A launch campaign, a shoot day or a website, where the client wants the final price on paper before agreeing.
  • Ad budget top-ups, if your agency pays for ads and recovers the money, and the client wants an estimate first.

What a proforma is not for: your regular monthly retainer. If a client has been with you for six months and pays every month, sending a proforma on the 1st and a tax invoice later doubles your paperwork for nothing. Issue the tax invoice on the 1st, and let the client pay against that.

What happens to GST when a client pays against a proforma?

This is where agencies get caught. For services, the time of supply under Section 13 of the CGST Act is the earlier of two dates: the date of the invoice (if you issue it within the time allowed), or the date you receive payment (GST Council flyer on time of supply). The time allowed for a service invoice is 30 days from the supply, under Rule 47 (CBIC invoice rules).

A proforma is not an invoice, so it doesn't count for that test. That means: the moment the client pays against your proforma, GST is due for that month, even if the work starts next month. Goods businesses have an exemption on advances; service businesses don't.

Example. You send a proforma for a ₹40,000 + 18% GST December retainer on 24 November. The client pays ₹47,200 on 27 November. Work starts 1 December.

GST of ₹7,200 is due with your November GSTR-3B, because payment came in November. On 27 November you issue either a receipt voucher (Rule 50) or the tax invoice itself. If you issue a receipt voucher now, you issue the tax invoice when the December work is billed and adjust the GST already paid.

Small agencies usually find it simpler to issue the tax invoice the day the money arrives and skip the receipt voucher. Ask your CA which they prefer for your books. The full treatment of advances, including what to do when a client cancels after paying, is in our guide to GST on advance payment for agency retainers.

The proforma doesn't create GST. The payment does. Book it the day the money lands, not the day the work starts.

What should a proforma invoice include?

There's no prescribed format, but a proforma that turns cleanly into a tax invoice has:

  1. The heading "Proforma invoice" and the line "This is not a tax invoice".
  2. A separate number, such as PI-2026-014, and a "valid until" date.
  3. Your name, address and GSTIN; the client's name, address and GSTIN if they have one.
  4. The services, the period, and the SAC code you'll use on the tax invoice (998361 for most agency work).
  5. The amount, GST shown as CGST + SGST or IGST, and the total.
  6. If the client deducts TDS, the expected net payable, so the PO is raised for the right amount. Our TDS guide has the rates.
  7. Bank and UPI details, and the line "Tax invoice will be issued on receipt of payment".

How do you convert a proforma into a tax invoice?

  1. Wait for the payment, or for the service to start, whichever is first.
  2. Issue a tax invoice from your normal series, with the same amounts as the proforma unless something changed.
  3. Mention the proforma number and the PO number in the notes, so the client's accounts team can match it.
  4. Record the payment against the tax invoice, not the proforma.
  5. Report the tax invoice in that month's GSTR-1. The proforma never appears in any return.

Five proforma mistakes agencies make

  1. Using the tax invoice series for proformas. When a proforma doesn't convert, you have a gap in your invoice numbers.
  2. Treating the proforma as the invoice. The client pays, nobody issues a tax invoice, and the GST for that month is short.
  3. Waiting for the work to start before paying GST on a services advance.
  4. Cancelling a tax invoice instead of issuing a credit note when a client changes the scope. Once issued, a tax invoice is corrected with a credit or debit note.
  5. Sending proformas every month to regular retainer clients, which doubles the paperwork and confuses their accounts team.

Frequently asked questions

Is GST payable on a proforma invoice?

No. A proforma is a quote, so sending one creates no GST liability and it does not go into your GSTR-1. But for services, GST becomes due when the client pays, even if the payment was made against a proforma. At that point issue a receipt voucher or the tax invoice and pay GST for that month.

Can a client claim input tax credit on a proforma invoice?

No. Input tax credit needs a valid tax invoice, and a proforma is not one. If your client's accounts team books your proforma as a purchase, their credit will not match your GSTR-1 and they will come back asking for the real invoice. Always follow a paid proforma with a tax invoice.

Should a proforma invoice use the same number series as tax invoices?

No. Keep proformas in their own series, for example PI-001, and print "Proforma invoice, not a tax invoice" on top. Your tax invoices must run in one unbroken series for the financial year. Mixing proformas into that series leaves gaps when a proforma never turns into a sale.
