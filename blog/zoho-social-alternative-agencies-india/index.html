Zoho Social Alternative for Agencies in India (Honest Take)

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published September 7, 2026

Indian agencies leave Zoho Social for three reasons that have nothing to do with the dollar: business plans hold one brand, content approvals start only at Premium, and client logins arrive only on the Agency plan at 10 brands. The best alternative depends on which of those three walls you have hit.

This is not the usual "Zoho alternative" post, because the usual argument does not apply. Zoho is headquartered in Chennai, bills Indian customers in rupees, and puts GST on its own invoice like any domestic vendor. The reverse-charge headache we walked through in the Hootsuite post is not Zoho's problem. So if you are searching for an alternative, it is because of how the product is shaped, not where it is billed from. Having run client work through Zoho Social before we built our own tool, I think that shape is worth describing precisely, because most of the competing lists are written by vendors who have clearly never opened the pricing page.

Read the brands column and the picture is clear. A brand in Zoho's language is one client. The four business plans each hold exactly one. Then there is nothing until ten. That gap is where almost every small Indian agency lives.

Why do agencies leave Zoho Social?

1. The one-to-ten cliff

A typical agency at the stage where it is shopping for software has three to six retainer clients. On Zoho Social, that agency has two choices. Buy Professional and bolt on brands one at a time, capped at five, with no approval workflow at all. Or buy Premium for the approvals and keep bolting on brands, each at its own per-brand price, with three seats for the whole team. Either way the monthly bill climbs with every client won, which is the pricing shape we argued against in the SocialPilot comparison: an agency's tool cost should not be a tax on growth. The jump to Agency at ten brands fixes the per-brand creep, but it is a big step for a team that needs it for four clients.

2. Approvals start at Premium, and the client is not in them

Content approvals and workflow appear on Premium and above. On Standard and Professional there is no approval step; the person who schedules is the person who publishes. Even on Premium, the approval is a team workflow. A client-side login, with a view built for the client rather than the agency, is an Agency-plan feature. So the moment your process needs the brand manager to approve on their phone before anything goes live, which in India means the moment you have a client at all, you are shopping at the top of the ladder. We wrote up what a working approval loop looks like in the faster client approvals post; Zoho can do it, but only on the plan sized for twenty clients.

3. It stops at publishing

Zoho Social does scheduling, listening, inbox and reports, and does them competently. It does not invoice your client, track a retainer, apply GST or note the TDS the client deducted. Zoho's answer is Zoho Books or Zoho Invoice, which are good products with their own login, their own subscription and their own idea of what a client is. The link between "this post was approved" and "this month's invoice went out" is a human, usually the founder, usually on a Sunday. Our GST invoicing post covers what that invoice has to contain; the point here is that on the Zoho path it lives two products away from the calendar.

4. Seats

One team member on Standard and Professional, three on Premium, five on Agency and Agency Plus. A five-person agency with a designer, an editor, two account managers and the founder fills the Agency plan's seats before a single client login is counted. Add-on seats exist, but again the bill grows with the team.

What is Zoho Social still the right answer for?

I want to be fair, because the free plan alone earns it a place. If you are one person with one brand, Zoho's free tier at six channels with uncapped posts is the best free scheduler in the market, and we said so in the free tools post. If your agency already runs Zoho CRM and Zoho Desk, the Premium integration that turns social conversations into leads and tickets is a real advantage nobody else offers cheaply. And if you are a twenty-brand shop that wants a listening inbox alongside publishing, Agency Plus is priced sensibly for that scale. The tool is not bad. It is shaped for one brand or for twenty, and the years in between are where agencies are built.

How to choose, in one pass

  • One brand, no client: stay on Zoho Social free. Genuinely.
  • Two to six clients, approvals needed: this is the gap. Pick a tool where the client is a first-class object from the cheapest plan, not an add-on.
  • Client billing is a monthly mess: pick the one tool that puts the invoice next to the calendar, or accept two products and a spreadsheet.
  • You sell community management: Agorapulse, or stay on Zoho Premium if you are in the Zoho suite.
  • Twenty brands, Zoho CRM in the building: Zoho Social Agency Plus is a defensible choice. Do not let anyone talk you out of it with a dollar argument that does not apply.

The one test that cuts through every comparison: count how many places a single client's work lives at the end of the month. Calendar, approval, published post, report, invoice. If that number is one, the tool is doing the job we describe on the agency management software page. If it is three, the software is not the system; you are.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zoho Social good for a small agency in India?

It is a solid scheduler with a genuinely free tier and Indian billing. The problem is the plan ladder: business plans hold one brand, Professional allows at most five add-on brands, content approvals start only at Premium, and client logins arrive only on the Agency plan at 10 brands. A three-to-six client agency sits in the gap.

Does Zoho Social handle client invoicing and GST?

No. Zoho Social publishes, listens and reports. Invoicing the client, GST, TDS tracking and retainer collection live in a separate product such as Zoho Books or Zoho Invoice, with its own login and subscription. The stitching between them is your job, which is the reason most agencies end up with a spreadsheet as the real system.

What does Zoho Social's Agency plan include?

Per Zoho's pricing page, Agency holds 10 brands with 14 channels each and 5 team members, adds client invite, portal customisation, agency-branded reports, scheduled report emails, custom client views and free client logins. Agency Plus doubles brands to 20 with the same 5 seats. Extra brands come in packs of 5.

What is the cheapest Zoho Social alternative for Indian agencies?

For pure scheduling, Buffer and Zoho's own free plan are hard to beat at one brand. Once approvals and client billing enter the picture, My Digital Sevak at ₹999 a month flat for the whole team is the lowest all-in option we know of, followed by Sendible and SocialBee at around $29 a month for schedulers with agency features.
