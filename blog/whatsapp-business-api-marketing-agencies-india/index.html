WhatsApp Business API for marketing agencies in India: when to sell it, and how to run it

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running social media for 63+ brands · Published September 17, 2026

The WhatsApp Business API lets an agency run broadcasts, a shared team inbox and automated replies on a client's official WhatsApp number. It suits clients with large opted-in customer lists or heavy ad-driven chat volume. Meta charges per template message, needs clear opt-in, and should always sit in the client's own Meta account.

Search this keyword and almost every result is a WhatsApp provider selling itself: "get the API free", plans from a few hundred rupees a month, a table of the "best providers in India" written by one of the providers. What you won't find is an agency's view of the real questions — which clients actually need it, what it costs once Meta's charges are added, who should own the account, and which rules get a number blocked.

We use the API ourselves for automated WhatsApp alerts inside our own agency, and we've set it up for clients. Here's what I'd tell an agency owner before they add "WhatsApp marketing" to a proposal.

What is the WhatsApp Business API, and how is it different from the app?

There are two ways for a business to use WhatsApp. The WhatsApp Business app is the free app on a phone: a business profile, labels, quick replies and a catalogue, used by one or a few people. The WhatsApp Business Platform — what everyone calls "the API" — has no phone app of its own. You reach it through a provider's dashboard (Meta calls them Business Solution Providers) or through Meta's Cloud API directly.

Which clients actually need it?

Fewer than providers suggest. I'd recommend the API only when at least one of these is true:

  • The client has a large opted-in list — past buyers, students, patients — and a genuine reason to message them: new batches, restocks, order updates.
  • Ads are driving chats. Click-to-WhatsApp campaigns can produce more conversations in a day than one phone can handle, and the team needs a shared inbox.
  • Several staff answer the same number and the owner wants to see who replied and how fast.
  • Messages should fire automatically — an order confirmation, a class reminder, a payment link.

For a salon or a single-doctor clinic answering thirty chats a day, the free app on a dedicated number is simpler and costs nothing. Selling them an API plan is selling a tool, not a result.

How much does the WhatsApp Business API cost in India?

There are two layers, and clients usually only hear about one of them.

Meta's charges. Since 1 July 2025, Meta has used per-message pricing: it charges for each delivered template message, with the rate depending on the template's category (marketing, utility or authentication) and the recipient's country. Marketing templates are always charged. Utility templates — like an order update — are free when sent inside an open customer service window, which opens when the customer messages the business. When someone messages the business from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad or a Facebook Page button, Meta gives a 72-hour free entry point window. Meta raised India's marketing rate from 1 January 2026, and it launched local-currency billing in India the same day, with eligible businesses required to move to INR billing by 31 December 2026 to avoid delivery disruption. Rates change often, so always quote from Meta's current rate card, not a provider's blog post.

The provider's fee. On top of Meta's charges, providers charge a monthly platform fee for the dashboard, inbox and chatbot builder. Public plans from Indian providers start at a few hundred rupees a month and climb steeply with team seats and automation features. Some also add a markup per message — ask for this in writing.

For the agency, the clean model is: the client pays Meta and the provider directly on their own card; the agency charges for strategy, templates, campaign setup and reporting. If you pass message costs through your own invoice, you're carrying the client's usage risk and complicating your GST — see GST invoices for digital marketing services.

Who should own the WhatsApp account — the agency or the client?

Always the client. The WhatsApp Business Account, the phone number and the Meta business portfolio it sits in should belong to the client, with the agency given access as a partner. The number is the client's customer relationship; if it sits in the agency's account, ending the contract turns into a hostage situation. It's the same rule we follow for Instagram and Facebook access, covered in auto-posting without sharing passwords.

Two onboarding checks save a lot of pain. First, the number: ask the provider whether it supports connecting a number already running on the WhatsApp Business app (Meta now documents this) or whether the client needs a fresh number. Second, the display name has to match the client's business; a name that doesn't gets rejected.

What are the rules for WhatsApp marketing messages?

WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy is short and strict. The parts agencies break most often:

  1. Opt-in comes first. The policy says you may only contact people who have given you their number and have given opt-in permission to receive messages from you. Meta's opt-in guidance adds that the opt-in must clearly say the person is agreeing to messages and name the business. A checkbox on a lead form that says "I agree to receive updates from [Brand] on WhatsApp" works. An old Excel sheet from a trade fair does not.
  2. Every opt-out is honoured — on or off WhatsApp. If a customer tells the store staff to stop messaging them, that counts.
  3. Bots need a human exit. Automation is allowed, but the policy requires "prompt, clear, and direct escalation paths" to a person.
  4. Keep the profile accurate, with real support contact details.

Beyond the policy, two practical limits catch agencies out. Meta's systems rate-limit businesses whose quality stays low — lots of blocks and reports lower a number's quality rating. And Meta caps how many marketing templates a single person receives across all businesses; providers such as MSG91 document that messages over the cap fail with a "healthy ecosystem engagement" error. Blasting the same offer to everyone three times a week burns both. Contact lists are also personal data under India's data protection law — who collects the consent and who holds the list is covered in our DPDP Act guide for agencies.

Where WhatsApp fits alongside social media

Instagram and Facebook find new people. WhatsApp talks to people who already raised their hand. In the plans we build, a reel drives a click-to-WhatsApp chat; a coaching institute's batch announcement goes out as a broadcast to past enquiries who opted in; a coaching or real estate client measures leads by the chats that became visits. It belongs in the same monthly calendar as the posts, so the offer on Instagram and the broadcast on WhatsApp say the same thing on the same day.

The 30-second version

  • Most small local clients don't need the API; the free Business app is enough.
  • Recommend it for large opted-in lists, click-to-WhatsApp ads, team inboxes and automation.
  • Cost = Meta's per-message charges + the provider's platform fee. Quote Meta's current rate card.
  • The client owns the account, the number and the Meta portfolio; the agency is a partner.
  • Opt-in naming the business, honour every opt-out, give bots a human exit.

FAQ

Does a marketing agency need the WhatsApp Business API?

Only when a client needs broadcasts to thousands of opted-in customers, a shared inbox for several staff, automation, or click-to-WhatsApp ad follow-up at scale. A small local business answering a few dozen chats a day is usually better served by the free WhatsApp Business app on a dedicated number.

How is the WhatsApp Business API charged in India?

Since 1 July 2025 Meta charges per delivered template message, with rates set by category and the recipient's country. Marketing templates always cost money; utility templates sent inside an open customer service window are free. Your provider usually adds a monthly platform fee on top, so compare both parts.

Can an agency send WhatsApp promotions to a client's customer list?

Only to people who gave their number and opted in to hear from that business by name. WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy requires that opt-in and requires honouring every opt-out. A bought database or an old list with no recorded consent should never be uploaded, whatever the provider allows.
