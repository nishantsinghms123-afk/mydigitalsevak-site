Meta Verified for Business in India: Is It Worth It in 2026?

By Nishant Singh, founder of My Digital Sevak — written after running a social media agency across 63+ brands · Published October 23, 2026

It's worth it for brands that get impersonated, sell through DMs or WhatsApp, or can't afford to be locked out of their account. It isn't worth it as a reach booster, because it isn't one. Business verification now sits inside Meta One, from ₹639 a month in India. Most small brands only need the cheapest plan.

Every few weeks a client forwards me the same screenshot: a Meta notification offering them a blue tick. The question that follows is always some version of "Should we take this? Will our reach go up?" The honest answer is that the tick does real work for some brands and almost nothing for others. And as of September 2026 the product isn't even called Meta Verified any more.

Here's what changed, what each plan includes in rupees, and how we decide which clients should pay for it.

What happened to Meta Verified for business?

Meta first launched Meta Verified for businesses in India in July 2024. There were four plans, Standard, Plus, Premium and Max, starting at ₹639 a month for one app and going up to ₹30,000 a month for Facebook and Instagram together once the launch discount ended. Every plan included a verified badge, account support and impersonation protection. In India you could only buy it inside the iOS or Android app.

On 15 September 2026, Meta rolled out Meta One, a single subscription brand covering consumer plans, AI plans and business and creator bundles. The business bundles are now called Essential, Advanced, Expert and Max, and the verified badge comes with Essential. Coverage of the launch quotes Meta saying it is "bringing the best of Meta Verified into Meta One." Existing Meta Verified subscribers haven't been forced to switch yet. Meta also says plans, benefits, pricing and availability "may vary by region, by app, and by account," and that the rollout is gradual. So the plan you see in your client's app may not match this article exactly.

Does Meta Verified increase reach?

No, and this is the misunderstanding we spend the most time clearing up. Meta's Meta One announcement says nothing about any plan increasing the reach of your posts or Reels. The upper tiers add discovery features: better profile discovery, possible featuring in the Facebook feed, a bolder follow button on Reels, and automatic follow invitations to people who engage with your content. Those can help people who already see your content follow you. They don't change how many people see a Reel in the first place.

If a client's reach has dropped, ₹639 a month won't fix it. The causes are almost always on the content side: too many reposted or watermarked videos, long gaps between posts, or a format mix that leans on statics when the audience watches Reels. We cover the fixes in our guides to the best time to post on Instagram in India and trial Reels for brands. Fix the content first. Buy the badge for the reasons below.

Which brands should pay for verification?

We recommend it when at least one of these is true:

  1. The brand gets impersonated. Fake pages that copy a brand's name and photos and then collect advance payments are common in jewellery, real estate, travel, coaching and anything sold cash-on-delivery. Our jewellery brand guide has more on this. A tick gives customers a quick way to tell the real account from the copy, and impersonation protection gives you a faster route to getting copies taken down.
  2. Sales happen in DMs or on WhatsApp. When someone is about to pay ₹40,000 for a lehenga over chat, the badge (and the verified WhatsApp Business channel that now comes with Essential) removes a real moment of doubt.
  3. Losing the account would hurt. If a hacked or disabled account would stop the business, paying for a human to talk to is cheap insurance. Note that, going by the India lineup reported at launch, human support starts at Advanced, not Essential.
  4. The brand runs regular paid campaigns. Customers who click an ad and land on a verified profile hesitate less. That's our experience, not a published benchmark, but it's consistent across our D2C clients.

We advise against it for a neighbourhood café or a new brand with a few hundred followers that hopes the tick will make it look bigger. Customers rarely question a small local business's authenticity, and ₹7,668 a year is better spent on a shoot day.

Do agencies need Advanced or higher for scheduling and team access?

Advanced bundles a few tools that agencies already have: scheduled stories, exportable analytics, and Instagram access for several people without sharing the password. If your team already works in proper tools, don't pay Meta for the same thing on every client. A scheduler connected through Meta's official API already lets your team publish without the client's Instagram password, and your agency dashboard already tracks approvals and deadlines.

The Advanced feature that's genuinely new is links in Instagram posts and Reels. For an e-commerce client who sells straight from content, four clickable Reels a month can justify ₹2,099 on their own. Test it for two months and compare link clicks against the "link in bio" traffic you had before.

How should an agency set it up for a client?

The badge is the client's asset, so set it up that way:

  • The client pays, on the client's account. Buy it from a phone the client or their admin controls, on their payment method. When it's on the agency's card, renewals fail when the card changes, nobody knows who owns it, and it lapses quietly the month the relationship ends.
  • Rebrand first, verify second. When Meta Verified launched, verified accounts couldn't change their name, username or profile photo without verifying again. Before a client renames their handle, finish the rename, then subscribe.
  • Keep documents ready. Verification checks the business against official details. Get the name on the profile to match the registered business name or brand before you apply, so the check doesn't stall.
  • Ask about the receipt. Purchases go through the app store, so the receipt comes from Apple or Google, not Meta. Ask the client's CA how they want it booked before the first renewal.
  • Charge for your time, not the subscription. Setting up verification, sorting out documents and handling impersonation reports is real work. List it as a one-time add-on. Our agency add-on price list template shows how to price it.
  • Put the renewal on the calendar. Track it with the client's other recurring items in your client onboarding checklist so a lapse doesn't come as a surprise.

So, is it worth it?

For a brand that gets copied, sells through chat, or can't afford a week locked out of its account, Essential at ₹639 a month is one of the cheapest bits of risk protection in its marketing budget. Advanced is worth testing only if clickable Reels would earn money. Expert and Max are for big teams and big stakes. For everyone else, including the many brands quietly hoping the tick will fix their reach, the money is better spent on content. We give the same advice to D2C brands in Jaipur as we do in Mumbai.

FAQ

Does Meta Verified increase reach on Instagram or Facebook?

No. Meta's September 2026 Meta One announcement makes no claim that any plan raises the reach of your posts. Higher tiers add discovery features such as better profile discovery and a bolder follow button, but feed and Reels distribution still depends on the content. Treat the badge as a trust and support purchase.

How much does Meta Verified for business cost in India?

Business verification now sits inside Meta One. Indian coverage of the launch reported Essential at 639 rupees a month, Advanced at 2,099, Expert at 6,399 and Max at 20,990. Meta says pricing and availability vary by region, app and account, so check the price shown in your own app before budgeting.

Is Meta Verified the same as Meta One?

Not exactly. Meta One, launched for businesses on 15 September 2026, is the new subscription home that bundles the verified badge with other paid tools. Coverage quotes Meta saying it is bringing the best of Meta Verified into Meta One. Existing Meta Verified subscribers have not been forced to switch yet.

Should an agency pay for a client's Meta Verified subscription?

No. The subscription should be bought by the client, on the client's account and payment method, from a device the client controls. If it sits on the agency's card, ownership, renewals and the tax receipt all get messy, and the badge can lapse when the relationship ends. Bill your setup time separately.
