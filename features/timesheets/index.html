Why do retainer agencies need timesheets at all?

Most of the 60+ brands we handle at Advance Media Solution are on monthly retainers, and for a long time we did not track time because "the fee is fixed anyway". That was a mistake. A retainer tells you what a client pays; it says nothing about what they cost. Two clients on the same ₹25,000 package can take wildly different hours once revisions, extra shoots and late-night "small changes" pile up.

Time tracking answers three questions every agency founder should be able to answer:

  • Which clients are actually profitable? Hours per client against the fee, month after month. Our piece on agency profit margins in India shows why this matters.
  • Has the scope grown? When a client asks for more, you can show the hours instead of arguing from memory.
  • What can we bill on top? Website builds, ad hoc shoots and consulting are easier to charge by the hour, and that time belongs on the invoice.

How does time tracking work for the team?

Time lives on tasks, the same posts, edits, shoots and to-dos your team already works in through the team workspace. Nobody has to keep a second list.

  • Timer on any task. Start it when the work starts; a running timer stays visible until it is stopped.
  • Weekly timesheet. A grid of tasks against days. Type the hours in, and each row can be switched between billable and non-billable.
  • Submit and approve. Each person submits their week with an optional note. A manager approves it or sends it back with changes asked for. An approved week is locked.

How do I turn billable hours into an invoice?

  1. Set hourly rates onceIn Timesheets → Rates: a default for the agency, a rate per person, per client, or for one person on one client. The most specific rate wins.
  2. Open the client's invoiceIn the invoice editor, choose Add billable time. It lists the client's uninvoiced billable time for the period, with an option to use only approved timesheets.
  3. Pick how the lines readOne line per task, task by person, one line per person, or a single line. Hours × rate become ordinary invoice lines.
  4. Send it like any other invoiceGST and TDS are worked out as usual, and the client pays in their app. The time is now marked invoiced.

If some time has no rate yet, you are told how many hours are missing one before you bill. And if the invoice is later cancelled or deleted, its time is released and becomes billable again, so the same hour is never charged twice. Everything about the invoice itself, from CGST/SGST to Razorpay payments, is on the GST invoicing page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to track billable hours in an agency?

Track time on the same tasks the work happens in, not in a separate sheet. Mark each entry billable or non-billable, have a manager approve the week, and pull approved billable time straight into the invoice. That way the hours your team logs and the hours your client pays for are the same numbers.

Do retainer agencies need timesheets?

Usually, yes. A fixed retainer hides how many hours a client really takes. Tracking time shows which retainers are profitable, gives you evidence when scope grows, and lets you bill extra work such as a website build or an unplanned shoot by the hour instead of absorbing it.

Can the same hours be billed twice?

No. When billable time is added to an invoice, those entries are marked invoiced and will not show up for billing again. If that invoice is cancelled or deleted, the time is released and becomes billable once more, so nothing is lost or charged twice.

Can my team see hourly rates and billing amounts?

No. Team members see their own hours. Managers see everyone's hours and approve timesheets but not rupee amounts. Rates and billing values are visible only to the admin and the accounts head, and only the admin can add or change a rate.
